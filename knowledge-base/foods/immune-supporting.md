# Immune-Supporting Foods

## Overview
Immune-supporting foods fortify both innate and adaptive immunity through vitamins, minerals, and bioactive compounds that enhance white blood cell function, reduce oxidative stress, and maintain the gut-associated lymphoid tissue where much of the immune system operates.

## Key Foods

| Food | Key Nutrients | Primary Benefit | How to Prepare | Ayurvedic Note (Dosha Effect) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Garlic | Allicin, alliin, selenium, vitamin C | Potent antimicrobial; stimulates NK cell activity | Crush and let sit 10 min before cooking; eat raw for max benefit | Warming — balances Kapha and Vata, aggravates Pitta |
| Elderberries | Anthocyanins, vitamin C, flavonoids | Shortens duration and severity of viral infections | Simmer into syrup; avoid raw which can cause nausea | Cooling — balances Pitta, fine for Vata and Kapha |
| Shiitake mushrooms | Lentinan, beta-glucans, ergothioneine | Activates macrophages and T-cell immune response | Saute or simmer in soups; avoid eating raw | Neutral to slightly warming — balances all doshas |
| Citrus fruits (lemon, orange) | Vitamin C, flavonoids, hesperidin | Enhances neutrophil and phagocyte function | Eat whole fruit or squeeze fresh; avoid bottled juice with sugar | Sour — stimulates agni, balances Vata, fine for Pitta in moderation |
| Manuka honey | MGO (methylglyoxal), hydrogen peroxide | Topical and internal antibacterial; throat soothing | Eat raw by the spoonful; never heat above 104F | Slightly heating — balances Vata and Kapha |
| Red bell peppers | Vitamin C (3x oranges), vitamin A, B6 | Highest vitamin C content among common vegetables | Eat raw in salads or lightly stir-fry; boiling loses 50% vitamin C | Slightly sweet — balances Vata and Pitta |
| Bone broth | Collagen, glutamine, minerals | Supports gut barrier which houses 70% of immune tissue | Simmer 12-24h with ACV; strain and sip warm | Balances Vata, may increase Kapha in excess |
| Oysters | Zinc, selenium, vitamin D, B12 | Zinc is essential for T-cell development and function | Steam or serve raw (if high quality); do not overcook | Cooling — balances Pitta, fine for Vata and Kapha |
| Astragalus root | Astragalosides, polysaccharides | Adaptogen that strengthens wei qi (defensive immunity) | Simmer dried root into soups or decoctions 20-30 min | Warming — strongly balances Vata, use cautiously in Pitta |
| Sauerkraut (raw) | Lactobacillus, vitamin C, vitamin K2 | Probiotic diversity strengthens mucosal immunity | Eat raw, unpasteurized, 2-4 tbsp with meals | Sour — stimulates agni, balances Kapha |

## Preparation Tips
- Crush garlic and let it rest 10 minutes before any heat exposure — this activates alliinase which converts alliin to allicin, the primary antimicrobial compound.
- Use raw Manuka honey — heating above 104F destroys methylglyoxal (MGO) and hydrogen peroxide activity that provide antibacterial benefits.
- Simmer shiitake mushrooms for at least 20 minutes to fully extract beta-glucans into the broth; short cooking leaves most locked in the cell walls.
- Eat red bell peppers raw or minimally cooked — vitamin C begins degrading at temperatures above 70F and is lost rapidly with prolonged heat.
- Avoid boiling citrus for teas — steep instead to preserve vitamin C and flavonoids.

## When to Eat
Immune-supportive foods are most effective as daily preventives rather than reactive treatments. Garlic is best consumed raw with meals. Elderberry syrup is traditionally taken at the first sign of illness and continued for 3-5 days. Astragalus is best used as a long-term tonic during cold and flu season, not during acute infections. Vitamin C-rich foods should be consumed in divided doses throughout the day since the body absorbs limited amounts at one time.

## Cautions
Elderberries must be cooked before consumption — raw elderberries contain cyanogenic glycosides (sambunigrin) which can cause nausea and gastrointestinal distress. Astragalus should not be used during acute febrile illnesses as it may "trap" the pathogen by strengthening defenses prematurely. Those with autoimmune conditions should use immune-stimulating herbs (astragalus, echinacea) cautiously, as they may exacerbate hyperactive immune responses.
