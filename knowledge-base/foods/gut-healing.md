# Gut-Healing Foods

## Overview
Gut-healing foods repair the intestinal lining, restore beneficial microbiome diversity, and support optimal digestion and nutrient absorption. Since approximately 70% of the immune system resides in the gut, these foods are foundational to overall health.

## Key Foods

| Slippery elm | Mucilage, antioxidants | Coats and soothes inflamed GI tract | Mix powder with warm water; steep as tea | Cooling and demulcent — balances Pitta and Vata |

## Preparation Tips
- Never heat fermented foods above 110F — pasteurization kills the live probiotics that make them therapeutic.
- Soak and properly cook legumes with kombu seaweed to reduce lectins and oligosaccharides that irritate the gut.
- Cook collagen-rich foods slowly at low temperatures; high heat denatures gelatin and reduces bioavailability.
- Add slippery elm or marshmallow root powder to cool or room-temperature liquids — hot water thickens it unpleasantly.
- Peel and deseed vegetables during acute gut healing phases to reduce insoluble fiber load on a compromised gut.

## When to Eat
Bone broth is best consumed first thing in the morning or 30 minutes before meals to prepare the gut lining. Fermented foods should accompany meals to support digestion. Prebiotic fibers (Jerusalem artichoke, garlic, onions) are best eaten at dinner when digestive capacity is highest. During active gut healing protocols, eat smaller, more frequent meals rather than three large ones.

## Cautions
Introduce fermented foods gradually — too much too fast can trigger die-off reactions (Herxheimer) and worsen bloating. Those with SIBO (Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth) should limit prebiotic fibers and focus on low-FODMAP options until bacterial overgrowth is addressed. Raw garlic and onions, while prebiotic, can irritate an inflamed gut lining during acute phases.
