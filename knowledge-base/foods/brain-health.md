# Brain Health Foods

## Overview
Brain-health foods provide essential compounds that support cognitive function, neurotransmitter production, neuroplasticity, and protection against oxidative stress—crucial for mental clarity, memory, focus, and long-term neurological resilience.

## Key Foods
| Food | Key Nutrients | Primary Benefit | How to Prepare | Ayurvedic Note (Dosha Effect) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wild-caught salmon | Omega-3s (DHA/EPA), vitamin D | Builds brain cell membranes, reduces inflammation | Raw (sashimi), poached, or lightly baked | Balances all doshas, especially Vata |

| Turmeric (with black pepper) | Curcumin, aromatics | Crosses blood-brain barrier, reduces neuroinflammation | Golden milk, add to savory dishes | Balances all doshas, reduces Kapha and Vata |

## Preparation Tips
- Always pair brain-supporting fats (omega-3s, avocados) with antioxidant-rich foods to enhance absorption and synergistic effects
- Cook turmeric with black pepper and healthy fats to increase curcumin absorption by up to 2000%
- Eat berries and other antioxidant foods on an empty stomach or between meals for maximum polyphenol uptake
- Preserve choline in eggs by avoiding high-heat cooking—soft boiling or poaching is optimal
- Activate pumpkin seeds and walnuts by soaking 8-12 hours to reduce phytic acid and improve nutrient bioavailability

## When to Eat
Brain foods are most beneficial in the morning to support cognitive function throughout the day. Include omega-3 sources daily, antioxidant-rich berries 3-4 times weekly. For evening focus, consume tryptophan and magnesium-rich foods (pumpkin seeds) 2 hours before bed. Seasonal focus: warming spices and cooked foods in colder months, raw antioxidant foods in summer.

## Cautions
Excess fish consumption may increase mercury exposure—choose smaller, wild-caught fish (sardines, wild salmon) over larger predatory fish. Those with histamine intolerance should moderate fermented and aged foods. High doses of omega-3 supplements may interact with blood thinners—food sources are generally safe. Individuals with dairy sensitivities should be cautious with processed cheeses often touted for choline content.
