# Nutrient-Dense Foods

## Overview
Nutrient-dense foods deliver the highest concentration of vitamins, minerals, and phytonutrients per calorie, filling nutritional gaps that are common even in well-planned diets. These foods form the foundation of any effective holistic nutrition protocol.

## Key Foods

| Food | Key Nutrients | Primary Benefit | How to Prepare | Ayurvedic Note (Dosha Effect) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Organ meats (liver) | Vitamin A (retinol), B12, folate, iron, copper | Most concentrated natural source of bioavailable vitamins | Pan-sear lightly or make pate; avoid overcooking | Warming — strongly builds Rakta dhatu (blood), balances Vata |
| Sardines | DHA, EPA, calcium (bones), B12, selenium, vitamin D | Complete omega-3 and mineral package in one food | Eat canned in olive oil or grill fresh with herbs | Balances Vata and Pitta; Kapha should limit |
| Egg yolks (pasture-raised) | Choline, lutein, zeaxanthin, B12, vitamin D | Choline critical for brain and liver; most bioavailable nutrients | Soft-boil or poach; avoid high-heat scrambling | Builds all dhatus; balances Vata and Pitta |
| Spirulina | Phycocyanin, iron, B vitamins, GLA | Complete protein with rare blue pigment antioxidant | Blend into smoothies; do not heat — destroys phycocyanin | Cooling — balances Pitta, use cautiously in Vata |
| Sweet potatoes | Vitamin A (beta-carotene), vitamin C, potassium, manganese | Efficient beta-carotene absorption with fats | Bake or roast with fat; steaming preserves more vitamin C | Sweet and grounding — balances Vata, may aggravate Kapha |
| Kale | Vitamin K1, vitamin C, manganese, lutein | Highest nutrient-to-calorie ratio among leafy greens | Massage raw with olive oil or lightly steam; avoid boiling | Bitter and astringent — balances Kapha and Pitta |
| Black beans | Folate, magnesium, iron, molybdenum, fiber | High folate and mineral content with plant protein | Soak overnight, cook with kombu; pressure cook for speed | Balances all doshas when well-spiced |
| Seaweed (nori, wakame) | Iodine, iron, folate, magnesium, trace minerals | Richest source of trace minerals from the ocean | Add to soups or salads; soak dried seaweed before use | Cooling — balances Pitta, use in moderation for Vata |
| Brazil nuts | Selenium, magnesium, vitamin E | 1-2 nuts provide 100% daily selenium need | Eat raw; do not roast — heat degrades selenium | Balances Vata and Pitta, may increase Kapha |
| Amaranth | Iron, magnesium, phosphorus, manganese, complete protein | Complete protein grain with superior mineral profile | Simmer 2:1 water ratio; can be popped like popcorn | Light and warming — balances all doshas |

## Preparation Tips
- Cook organ meats lightly (medium-rare) — overcooking destroys heat-sensitive vitamins A and B12 and makes the texture unpleasant.
- Pair beta-carotene rich foods (sweet potatoes, carrots) with a fat source to increase vitamin A conversion by up to 600%.
- Soak and sprout grains and legumes to reduce phytic acid by 50-80%, dramatically improving mineral absorption.
- Store leafy greens with a damp paper towel in a breathable bag — wilting reduces vitamin C content by up to 25% per day.
- Use canned sardines with bones left in — the softened bones are the best dietary source of bioavailable calcium.

## When to Eat
Nutrient-dense foods should be distributed across all meals for sustained nourishment. Organ meats are best consumed 1-2 times per week rather than daily due to vitamin A's fat-soluble accumulation potential. Seaweed is most beneficial in small daily amounts (1-2 sheets of nori). Sardines and eggs make excellent breakfast protein sources. Leafy greens are best consumed at lunch when digestive fire (agni) is strongest in Ayurvedic tradition.

## Cautions
Liver is extremely rich in preformed vitamin A (retinol) — excessive intake during pregnancy can be teratogenic; limit to 1-2 servings per week. Brazil nuts are so selenium-rich that eating more than 4-5 daily can cause selenium toxicity (selenosis). Those with hemochromatosis or iron overload should be cautious with organ meats and spirulina. Oxalate-sensitive individuals (kidney stones) should moderate spinach and certain seaweeds.
