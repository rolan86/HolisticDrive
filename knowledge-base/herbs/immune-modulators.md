## Key Principles
- **Acute vs. chronic use** — Echinacea/Elderberry for acute illness; Astragalus/Reishi for long-term support
- **Immune stimulation vs. modulation** — some boost (Echinacea), others regulate (Reishi, Guduchi)
- **Seasonal timing** — immune tonics best started before cold/flu season
- **Contraindicated in autoimmunity** — immune-stimulating herbs may exacerbate conditions
- **Synergistic combinations** — pair immunomodulators with anti-inflammatories for balanced support

## Cautions
- **Autoimmune conditions** — most immune-stimulating herbs contraindicated without professional guidance
- **Immunosuppressant interactions** — may reduce efficacy of transplant medications, corticosteroids
- **Not all are safe in pregnancy** — Elderberry, Neem, Holy Basil require caution
- **Timing matters** — immunostimulants best for acute illness; avoid during active infection with fever
- **Elderberry safety** — NEVER consume raw elderberries (cyanogenic glycosides cause toxicity)
- **Neem potency** — very bitter and cooling; contraindicated in pregnancy, may cause liver toxicity at high doses
- **Reishi bleeding risk** — mild anticoagulant effects; discontinue before surgery
- **Drug interactions** — may affect chemotherapy, diabetes medications, anticoagulants
