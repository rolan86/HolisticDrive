# Turmeric (Curcuma longa)

## Profile
- **Category:** Anti-inflammatory, Antioxidant, Gut herb
- **Ayurvedic:** Bitter, pungent, astringent rasa; heating virya; pungent vipaka; pacifies Kapha and Pitta, aggravates Vata in excess

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Strong
- **Key studies:** Hewlings & Kalman, J Med Food, 2017 (meta-analysis on osteoarthritis); Daily et al., J Clin Psychopharmacol, 2008 (RCT, depression); Kuptniratsaikul et al., Clin Interv Aging, 2014 (RCT, knee OA)

## Key Compounds
- Curcumin (diferuloylmethane)
- Demethoxycurcumin
- Bisdemethoxycurcumin
- Turmerones (ar-turmerone, alpha-turmerone)
- Essential oils (zingiberene, curcumene)

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 500-2000 mg standardized curcumin extract (95% curcuminoids), 1-2 times daily
- **Form:** Capsule (with piperine for enhanced bioavailability), powder, decoction
- **Duration:** Anti-inflammatory effects noticeable within 4-8 weeks; long-term use considered safe up to 2 years

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Avoid in bile duct obstruction and gallstones
- Use cautiously in GERD and peptic ulcers (may exacerbate at high doses)
- Caution in pregnancy (traditional use for uterine tone, limit culinary amounts)
- Discontinue 2 weeks before surgery (bleeding risk)
- May cause GI discomfort at doses above 2000 mg/day

## Drug Interactions
- Anticoagulants and antiplatelets (warfarin, clopidogrel) — increased bleeding risk
- Diabetes medications (metformin, insulin) — enhanced blood sugar lowering
- Antacids and acid reducers — may alter absorption
- Chemotherapy agents — potential interactions, consult oncologist
- Immunosuppressants — may reduce efficacy due to immune-modulating effects
