# Guduchi (Tinospora cordifolia)

## Profile
- **Category:** Immunomodulator, Adaptogen, Anti-inflammatory
- **Ayurvedic:** Bitter-astringent (tikta-kashaya) rasa, heating (ushna) virya, sweet (madhura) vipaka; balances all three doshas (tridoshic), especially Vata and Pitta.

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Strong
- **Key studies:**
  - Chintalwar G et al., Phytomedicine, 2003 — immunomodulatory activity of Tinospora cordifolia in human polymorphonuclear cells.
  - Bafna M & Mishra S.H., Journal of Ethnopharmacology, 2005 — anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties of Guduchi stem validated in animal models.
  - Sharma V et al., Journal of Ayurveda and Integrative Medicine, 2012 — clinical trial showed Guduchi enhanced immune markers in allergic rhinitis patients.

## Key Compounds
- Berberine
- Tinosporide
- Cordifolioside A and B
- Diterpenoid lactones
- Syringin

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 300-500 mg of standardized stem extract, 2-3 times daily; 1-3 g of stem powder (Guduchi Satva).
- **Form:** Powder (churna), tablet, capsule, decoction (kwath), and Guduchi Satva (starch extract).
- **Duration:** Immune modulation noticeable within 2-4 weeks; best results at 8-12 weeks. Well-tolerated for long-term rasayana use under guidance.

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Use cautiously in autoimmune conditions — immunostimulant properties may exacerbate disease activity.
- Caution in patients with known hypoglycemia due to blood sugar lowering effects.
- Avoid in acute diarrhea or IBS-D, as it can increase bowel motility.
- Reduce dose during acute febrile illness; allow the body's natural response.
- Pregnancy safety not well established; avoid or use only under qualified practitioner supervision.

## Drug Interactions
- Immunosuppressants (e.g., methotrexate, cyclosporine) — may reduce efficacy due to immunostimulant action.
- Antidiabetic drugs — may enhance hypoglycemic effect; monitor blood sugar closely.
- Anticoagulants and antiplatelets — may increase bleeding risk; monitor INR if on warfarin.
- Berberine-containing herbs — cumulative berberine load may affect CYP3A4 metabolism.
- See herb-drug.md for full details.
