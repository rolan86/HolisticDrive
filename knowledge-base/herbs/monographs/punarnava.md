# Punarnava (Boerhavia diffusa)

## Profile
- **Category:** Diuretic, Anti-inflammatory, Renal Tonic
- **Ayurvedic:** Sweet-bitter-astringent (madhura-tikta-kashaya) rasa, heating (ushna) virya, pungent (katu) vipaka; pacifies Kapha and Vata, may aggravate Pitta in excess.

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Moderate
- **Key studies:**
  - Agarwal V et al., Phytomedicine, 2009 — diuretic, nephroprotective, and anti-inflammatory activities of Boerhavia diffusa root extract in rats.
  - Sharma V et al., Drug Invention Today, 2013 — review of pharmacological activities with emphasis on renoprotective and hepatoprotective properties.
  - Gupta S.C. et al., Journal of Ethnopharmacology, 2016 — clinical study demonstrated significant reduction in proteinuria in patients with nephrotic syndrome.

## Key Compounds
- Punarnavoside
- Punarnavine
- Boeravinones (A through G)
- Liriodendron
- Ursolic acid

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 250-500 mg of standardized root extract, 2-3 times daily; 1-3 g of dried root powder.
- **Form:** Powder (churna), capsule, tablet, decoction (kwath), fresh juice (swarasa).
- **Duration:** Diuretic effects within days; renal and anti-inflammatory benefits at 4-8 weeks. Not recommended for continuous use beyond 8-12 weeks without breaks.

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Avoid in pregnancy — diuretic and uterine-stimulant properties noted in animal studies.
- Use cautiously in severe dehydration or electrolyte imbalance.
- Not suitable for individuals with high Pitta aggravation (bleeding disorders, hyperacidity).
- Caution in patients with kidney stones — increased urine flow may mobilize stones; ensure adequate hydration.
- Monitor potassium levels with prolonged use due to diuretic effect.

## Drug Interactions
- Diuretics (furosemide, hydrochlorothiazide) — additive diuretic effect; risk of electrolyte depletion and dehydration.
- Antihypertensive medications — enhanced blood pressure lowering; may require dose adjustment.
- Lithium — diuretic effect may increase lithium levels to toxicity; avoid combination.
- NSAIDs — combined use may increase renal strain; monitor renal function.
- Antidiabetic medications — may alter blood glucose levels; monitor closely.
