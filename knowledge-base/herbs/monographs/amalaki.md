# Amalaki (Emblica officinalis)

## Profile
- **Category:** Rasayana (rejuvenative) / Antioxidant
- **Ayurvedic:** All five rasas except salty (sweet, sour, bitter, pungent, astringent); cool virya; sweet vipaka; tridoshic — pacifies all three doshas

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Moderate
- **Key studies:** Bhattacharya A et al., British Journal of Nutrition, 2011 (antioxidant status in diabetic patients); Poltanov EA et al., Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry, 2009 (antioxidant and anti-inflammatory); Mehta N et al., Pharmacognosy Reviews, 2017 (comprehensive pharmacological review)

## Key Compounds
- Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) — exceptionally high and heat-stable due to tannin complexing
- Emblicanin A and B — tannin-bound antioxidants unique to Amalaki
- Ellagic acid — anti-cancer, hepatoprotective
- Gallic acid — antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory
- Punigluconin and pedunculagin — hydrolyzable tannins with antioxidant activity

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 500–1,000 mg of standardized fruit extract twice daily; or 3–6 g of churna (dried fruit powder) with warm water
- **Form:** Churna (powder), capsule, tablet, fresh fruit juice, churna (part of Triphala formulation)
- **Duration:** Antioxidant effects are relatively rapid (within 1–2 weeks); rejuvenative benefits are cumulative over months; safe for long-term daily use

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Avoid in acute diarrhea — may have a laxative effect at higher doses
- Use with caution in patients on antidiabetic medications — may enhance hypoglycemic effect
- May increase bleeding risk at very high doses — discontinue before surgery
- Avoid in patients with severe kidney disease (high oxalate content)
- Pregnancy: generally regarded as safe in food amounts; therapeutic doses should be supervised
- May cause loose stools in sensitive individuals

## Drug Interactions
- Antidiabetic medications (metformin, insulin) — may enhance blood sugar lowering
- Anticoagulants/antiplatelets — possible additive effect due to vitamin C and antioxidant content
- Iron supplements — vitamin C enhances non-heme iron absorption (beneficial interaction)
- Statins — may have synergistic lipid-modulating effects
- Chemotherapy agents — antioxidant may interfere with pro-oxidant mechanism of some chemo drugs
