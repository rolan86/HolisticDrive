# Kutki (Picrorhiza kurroa)

## Profile
- **Category:** Hepatoprotective, Anti-inflammatory, Bitter Tonic
- **Ayurvedic:** Bitter (tikta) rasa, cooling (shita) virya, pungent (katu) vipaka; pacifies Pitta and Kapha, reduces Vata when used with anupana.

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Strong
- **Key studies:**
  - Shukla B et al., Planta Medica, 1992 — Picrorhiza kurroa root extract showed potent hepatoprotective activity comparable to silymarin against galactosamine-induced liver damage.
  - Dahanukar S.A. et al., Journal of Postgraduate Medicine, 2000 — clinical trial demonstrated efficacy in acute viral hepatitis with faster normalization of liver enzymes.
  - Pandey R et al., Phytotherapy Research, 2019 — kutkoside and picroside I demonstrated significant anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects in asthma models.

## Key Compounds
- Picroside I and II
- Kutkoside
- Kutkin
- Apocynin
- Vanillic acid

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 200-400 mg of standardized root extract, 2-3 times daily; 1-2 g of dried root powder.
- **Form:** Powder (churna), capsule, tablet, decoction (kwath), or mixed with honey as anupana.
- **Duration:** Hepatoprotective effects visible in 2-4 weeks via liver enzyme normalization. Anti-inflammatory and immune effects at 4-8 weeks. Optimal at 3-6 month courses with periodic evaluation.

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Avoid during pregnancy and lactation due to insufficient safety data and bitter digestive stimulation.
- Caution in patients with hypoglycemia — blood sugar lowering effects reported.
- May cause loose stools or mild GI discomfort at higher doses due to bitter cholagogue action.
- Not recommended for individuals with severe coldness or weakness (vata-kapha dominance) without warming herbs.
- Rare risk of allergic skin rash; discontinue if occurs.

## Drug Interactions
- Antidiabetic medications — may enhance hypoglycemic effects; monitor blood glucose.
- Immunosuppressants — immunomodulatory action may reduce drug efficacy.
- Anticoagulants — potential additive effects on bleeding; monitor INR.
- Cytochrome P450 substrates — apocynin may inhibit certain CYP enzymes, altering drug metabolism.
- Hepatotoxic drugs — hepatoprotective effect may mask liver damage markers; avoid reliance on kutki alone.
