# Arjuna (Terminalia arjuna)

## Profile
- **Category:** Cardioprotective, Antihypertensive, Astringent
- **Ayurvedic:** Astringent (kashaya) rasa, cooling (shita) virya, pungent (katu) vipaka; pacifies Pitta and Kapha, may aggravate Vata in excess.

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Strong
- **Key studies:**
  - Dwivedi S., Indian Heart Journal, 2007 — Terminalia arjuna bark powder improved left ventricular function in ischemic heart disease patients in a double-blind crossover trial.
  - Kapoor D et al., Indian Journal of Clinical Biochemistry, 2014 — cardioprotective mechanisms of arjuna include antioxidant, anti-ischemic, and lipid-lowering effects.
  - Kumar S et al., Phytomedicine, 2019 — systematic review confirmed T. arjuna as safe adjunct therapy for chronic stable angina.

## Key Compounds
- Arjunic acid
- Arjungenin
- Arjunolone
- Terminalin
- Oleanolic acid derivatives

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 500 mg of standardized bark extract, 2-3 times daily; 1-3 g of bark powder.
- **Form:** Bark powder (churna), capsule, tablet, decoction (kwath), Arjuna Ksheerapaka (milk decoction).
- **Duration:** Cardiovascular benefits emerge over 4-12 weeks; lipid improvements at 8-12 weeks. Safe for long-term use under supervision. Do not discontinue cardiac medications without medical advice.

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Not a substitute for prescribed cardiac medications; always use as adjunct under medical supervision.
- Caution in patients with very low blood pressure — may cause further reduction.
- Avoid in severe liver disease due to altered metabolism.
- Discontinue 2 weeks before any scheduled surgery.
- Use cautiously in hypothyroid patients — some evidence of thyroid hormone modulation.

## Drug Interactions
- Antihypertensive drugs (ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers) — may enhance blood pressure lowering; dose adjustment may be needed.
- Anticoagulants and antiplatelets (warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel) — antiplatelet activity may increase bleeding risk; monitor INR.
- Statins and lipid-lowering agents — additive lipid-lowering effect; may allow dose reduction but requires medical oversight.
- Thyroid medications — may potentiate effects; monitor thyroid function tests.
