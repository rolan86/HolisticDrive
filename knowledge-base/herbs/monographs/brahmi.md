# Brahmi (Bacopa monnieri)

## Profile
- **Category:** Nootropic / Nervine / Adaptogen
- **Ayurvedic:** Bitter, astringent rasa; cool virya; sweet vipaka; pacifies Vata and Kapha, may aggravate Pitta in excess

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Strong (for cognitive function)
- **Key studies:** Stough C et al., Psychopharmacology, 2001 (memory and cognitive performance); Calabrese C et al., Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine, 2008 (memory in healthy elderly); Kongkeaw C et al., Journal of Ethnopharmacology, 2014 (meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials)

## Key Compounds
- Bacosides A and B (triterpenoid saponins) — primary cognitive-enhancing actives
- Bacopasaponins — neuroprotective, antioxidant
- Betulinic acid — anti-inflammatory, anti-cancer
- Brahmine — mild sedative (in high doses)
- Herpestine — alkaloid with cardiotonic properties

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 300–600 mg of standardized extract (50% bacosides) daily, divided into 2 doses
- **Form:** Capsule, tablet, tincture, powder (often taken with ghee in Ayurvedic tradition)
- **Duration:** Cognitive effects are gradual — significant improvement typically seen after 8–12 weeks of consistent use; safe for long-term daily use

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Avoid in pregnancy and lactation — insufficient safety data
- May cause mild GI upset, nausea, or dry mouth at higher doses
- Use with caution in patients with hyperthyroidism — may enhance thyroid hormone production
- May have additive sedative effects with CNS depressants
- Avoid in known allergy to Brahmi or Scrophulariaceae family

## Drug Interactions
- Thyroid medications (levothyroxine) — may enhance thyroid hormone levels; monitor TSH and T4
- CNS depressants (benzodiazepines, barbiturates, alcohol) — potential additive sedation
- Cholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil, rivastigmine) — theoretical synergism for Alzheimer's; coordinate with prescriber
- Anticholinergic drugs — may counteract brahmi's cholinergic activity
- Antihypertensives — may enhance blood pressure lowering

## Key Sources
- Stough C et al. "Neurocognitive effects of Bacopa monnieri in healthy human subjects." Psychopharmacology. 2001.
- Calabrese C et al. "Effects of a standardized Bacopa monnieri extract on cognitive performance, anxiety, and depression in the elderly." Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine. 2008.
- Kongkeaw C et al. "Meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials on cognitive effects of Bacopa monnieri extract." Journal of Ethnopharmacology. 2014.
