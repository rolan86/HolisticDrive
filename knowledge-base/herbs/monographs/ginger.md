# Ginger (Zingiber officinale)

## Profile
- **Category:** Antiemetic, Carminative, Anti-inflammatory, Circulatory stimulant
- **Ayurvedic:** Pungent rasa, heating virya, sweet vipaka; pacifies Vata and Kapha, aggravates Pitta in excess

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Strong
- **Key studies:** Betoni et al., Phytother Res, 2005 (meta-analysis on nausea); Viljoen et al., Nutrients, 2019 (systematic review, nausea and vomiting); Bartels et al., Pain, 2015 (RCT, osteoarthritis pain)

## Key Compounds
- Gingerols (6-gingerol)
- Shogaols (6-shogaol)
- Paradols
- Zingiberene
- Beta-bisabolene

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 1-2 g dried ginger powder or 250 mg standardized extract, 2-3 times daily
- **Form:** Capsule, fresh root (tea/decoction), tincture, crystallized ginger
- **Duration:** Anti-nausea effects within 30 minutes to 2 hours; anti-inflammatory benefits at 4-8 weeks; safe for short and long-term use

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Avoid in severe bleeding disorders (inhibits platelet aggregation)
- Use cautiously in gallstone disease (stimulates bile production)
- Caution in GERD at high doses (may worsen heartburn)
- Avoid high doses in pregnancy beyond the first trimester without supervision
- Reduce dose if experiencing heartburn or acid reflux

## Drug Interactions
- Anticoagulants (warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel) — increased bleeding risk
- Antidiabetic medications — enhanced blood sugar lowering
- Antihypertensives — may add to blood pressure-lowering effects
- Calcium channel blockers — potential additive effects on blood pressure
- NSAIDs — potential additive GI irritation at high doses
