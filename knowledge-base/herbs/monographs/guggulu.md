# Guggulu (Commiphora mukul)

## Profile
- **Category:** Anti-inflammatory / Lipid-modulating / Resin
- **Ayurvedic:** Pungent, bitter, astringent rasa; warm virya; pungent vipaka; pacifies Kapha and Vata, may aggravate Pitta

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Moderate
- **Key studies:** Szapary PO et al., Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism, 2003 (guggulipid vs. simvastatin in hypercholesterolemia); Singh RB et al., Cardiovascular Drugs and Therapy, 1994 (lipid-lowering trial); Nityanand S, Kapoor NK. "Hypolipidaemic effect of Commiphora mukul resin (guggulu)." Indian Journal of Experimental Biology, 1973

## Key Compounds
- Guggulsterones (E and Z isomers) — primary actives; lipid-modulating, thyroid-stimulating
- Commipheric acids — anti-inflammatory
- Myrrhanol A — anti-inflammatory
- Guggulipid (standardized extract) — lipid-regulating complex
- Volatile oils — antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 500–1,500 mg of standardized guggul extract (2.5% guggulsterones) twice daily
- **Form:** Capsule, tablet (often combined with Trikatu or Triphala in Ayurvedic formulations), purified guggulu resin
- **Duration:** Lipid effects typically within 4–12 weeks; anti-inflammatory effects within 2–4 weeks; safe for 3–6 months of continuous use; cycle with breaks

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Avoid in pregnancy — may stimulate uterine contractions
- Avoid in active liver disease — hepatotoxicity reported in rare cases
- Use with caution in thyroid disorders — guggulsterones stimulate thyroid function
- Avoid in acute diarrhea or dysentery
- Not suitable for Pitta-aggravated conditions without appropriate formulation (traditionally combined with Triphala)
- Discontinue 2 weeks before surgery due to bleeding risk

## Drug Interactions
- Warfarin and anticoagulants — may reduce anticoagulant effectiveness (induces CYP3A4)
- Thyroid medications (levothyroxine) — additive thyroid-stimulating effect; monitor thyroid function
- Statins and lipid-lowering drugs — potential additive effect; monitor lipid panels
- Beta-blockers — may reduce bioavailability of propranolol
- Diazepam — may accelerate metabolism via CYP3A4 induction
- Diltiazem — reduced plasma levels reported

- Singh RB et al. "Hypolipidemic and antioxidant effects of Commiphora mukul as an adjunct to dietary therapy in patients with hypercholesterolemia." Cardiovascular Drugs and Therapy. 1994.
- Urizar NL, Moore DD. "GUGULIPID: a natural cholesterol-lowering agent." Annual Review of Nutrition. 2003.
