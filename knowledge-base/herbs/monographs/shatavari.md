# Shatavari (Asparagus racemosus)

## Profile
- **Category:** Adaptogen / Rasayana / Reproductive tonic
- **Ayurvedic:** Sweet, bitter rasa; cool virya; sweet vipaka; pacifies Vata and Pitta, may aggravate Kapha in excess

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Moderate
- **Key studies:** Alok S et al., International Journal of Pharmaceutical & Biological Archives, 2013 (comprehensive pharmacological review); Parihar P et al., International Journal of Pharmaceutical Sciences, 2015 (galactagogue and phytoestrogen review); Thakur M et al., Phytomedicine, 2009 (adaptogenic and anti-stress activity)

## Key Compounds
- Shatavarins (steroidal saponins) — primary actives; galactagogue, anti-inflammatory
- Racemosol — antispasmodic, uterine relaxant
- Asparagine — diuretic
- Shatavarin IV — immunomodulatory
- Flavonoids (quercetin, rutin) — antioxidant, anti-inflammatory

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 500–1,000 mg of root extract twice daily; or 3–6 g of powdered root in warm milk (traditional preparation)
- **Form:** Capsule, powder (churna), tablet, decoction in milk
- **Duration:** Reproductive and adaptogenic benefits are typically gradual — expect effects within 4–8 weeks; safe for long-term use as a rasayana

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Avoid in estrogen-sensitive cancers (breast, ovarian, uterine) without oncologist supervision — phytoestrogenic activity
- Use with caution in patients with hormone-sensitive conditions (endometriosis, fibroids)
- May aggravate Kapha dosha — avoid in conditions of excess mucus or congestion
- Pregnancy: traditionally used to support healthy pregnancy but should be supervised; avoid in first trimester without guidance
- May cause mild GI upset at higher doses

## Drug Interactions
- Hormone replacement therapy — potential additive estrogenic effect
- Oral contraceptives — possible interaction via phytoestrogen activity
- Diuretics — may enhance diuretic effect
- Antidiabetic medications — may lower blood sugar
- Immunosuppressants — immunomodulatory effects may antagonize

- Parihar P et al. "Phytoestrogens from Asparagus racemosus: a review." International Journal of Pharmaceutical Sciences. 2015.
