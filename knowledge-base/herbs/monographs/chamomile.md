# Chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla)

## Profile
- **Category:** Nervine, Carminative, Anti-inflammatory, Mild sedative
- **Ayurvedic:** Bitter, sweet rasa; cooling virya; sweet vipaka; pacifies Pitta and Vata

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Moderate
- **Key studies:** Srivastava et al., Mol Med Rep, 2010 (review, anti-inflammatory); Mao et al., J Clin Psychopharmacol, 2016 (RCT, generalized anxiety); Akhondzadeh et al., J Clin Pharm Ther, 2011 (RCT, sleep quality)

## Key Compounds
- Apigenin
- Chamazulene
- Bisabolol (alpha-bisabolol)
- Matricin
- Flavonoids (quercetin, luteolin)

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 2-3 g dried flower heads as tea, 3-4 times daily; or 220-1100 mg standardized extract daily
- **Form:** Tea (infusion), capsule, tincture, topical preparations
- **Duration:** Anxiolytic and mild sedative effects within 1-2 weeks; GI benefits within days; safe for long-term use as a gentle tonic

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Avoid in severe ragweed or asteraceae allergy (cross-reactivity possible)
- Use cautiously in pregnancy (traditional use is generally safe in moderation)
- Caution with estrogen-sensitive conditions (phytoestrogenic activity reported)
- Discontinue 2 weeks before surgery (potential interaction with anticoagulants)
- Rare contact dermatitis with topical use

## Drug Interactions
- Anticoagulants (warfarin) — coumarin content may increase bleeding risk, monitor INR
- Sedatives and benzodiazepines — mild additive CNS depression
- Cyclosporine — apigenin may increase cyclosporine levels via CYP inhibition
- Oral contraceptives — theoretical interaction via estrogenic effects
- Antihypertensives — mild additive blood pressure lowering

## Key Sources
- Srivastava & Gupta, "Anti-inflammatory Effects of Chamomile," Mol Med Rep, 2010
- McKay & Blumberg, "A Review of the Bioactivity of Chamomile," Phytother Res, 2006
- Mao et al., "Chamomile for Generalized Anxiety Disorder," J Clin Psychopharmacol, 2016
