# Tagara (Valeriana jatamansi)

## Profile
- **Category:** Nervine, Sedative, Antispasmodic
- **Ayurvedic:** Bitter-pungent-astringent (tikta-katu-kashaya) rasa, heating (ushna) virya, pungent (katu) vipaka; pacifies Vata and Kapha, may aggravate Pitta at high doses.

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Moderate
- **Key studies:**
  - Pathak S et al., Phytomedicine, 2011 — Valeriana jatamansi rhizome extract showed significant anxiolytic and sedative activity in rodent models comparable to diazepam.
  - Sharma J et al., Journal of Ethnopharmacology, 2013 — neuroprotective and antioxidant effects of V. jatamansi demonstrated in ischemia-reperfusion injury models.
  - Bos R. et al., Planta Medica, 1998 — comparative analysis of valepotriates in V. jatamansi versus V. officinalis confirmed similar pharmacological profiles.

## Key Compounds
- Valepotriates
- Valerenic acid
- Jatamansin
- Patchouli alcohol
- Isovaleric acid

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 250-500 mg of standardized rhizome extract, 1-2 times daily; 1-3 g of dried rhizome powder.
- **Form:** Powder (churna), capsule, tablet, tincture, medicated oil (Taila), and as part of compound formulations (Manasamitra Vataka).
- **Duration:** Sedative and anxiolytic effects noticeable within first few doses. Optimal anxiolytic benefit at 2-4 weeks. Recommended to cycle use (2-4 weeks on, 1-2 weeks off) to prevent tolerance.

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Avoid in pregnancy — valepotriates have uterine-stimulant properties.
- Caution while driving or operating machinery — sedative effects may impair alertness.
- Avoid combining with alcohol or other CNS depressants without medical supervision.
- Caution in patients with depression — may worsen depressive symptoms in some individuals.
- Not recommended for children under 6; use with caution in adolescents.
- May cause vivid dreams or morning grogginess — reduce dose or take earlier in the evening.

## Drug Interactions
- Benzodiazepines and barbiturates — significant additive CNS depression; avoid combination or use only under medical supervision.
- Antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs, tricyclics) — may interact unpredictably; monitor mood changes closely.
- Antihistamines (first-generation) — additive sedative effect; caution with concurrent use.
- Sleep medications (zolpidem, eszopiclone) — enhanced sedation; risk of over-sedation.
- Hepatotoxic drugs — valepotriates may have mild hepatotoxic potential; monitor liver function.
