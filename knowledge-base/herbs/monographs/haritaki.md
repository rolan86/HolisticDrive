# Haritaki (Terminalia chebula)

## Profile
- **Category:** Gut herb / Rasayana (rejuvenative) / Laxative
- **Ayurvedic:** All five rasas (sweet, sour, bitter, pungent, astringent); warm virya; sweet vipaka; tridoshic but especially balancing for Vata; traditionally used in all six seasonal rasayana practices

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Moderate
- **Key studies:** Lee HS et al., Journal of Ethnopharmacology, 2015 (anti-obesity and metabolic effects); Chaudhary AK et al., Pharmacognosy Reviews, 2015 (comprehensive pharmacognosy review); Bag A et al., Journal of Ethnopharmacology, 2013 (antimicrobial activity)

## Key Compounds
- Chebulagic acid — antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anti-diabetic
- Chebulinic acid — laxative, hepatoprotective
- Corilagin — anti-inflammatory, anti-cancer
- Gallic acid — antimicrobial, antioxidant
- Terminalin — anti-diabetic, antioxidant
- Punicalagin — anti-inflammatory, astringent

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 500–1,000 mg of fruit extract once or twice daily; or 3–5 g of churna (powder) in warm water before bed
- **Form:** Churna (powder), capsule, tablet, decoction, Triphala constituent
- **Duration:** Laxative and digestive effects within hours to days; rejuvenative effects over months; safe for long-term use as a rasayana; traditional use includes seasonal cycling

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Avoid in acute diarrhea or dysentery — has laxative properties
- Avoid in pregnancy — traditional contraindication; may stimulate uterine contractions
- Use with caution in patients with severe dehydration or electrolyte imbalance
- May cause abdominal cramping at higher doses — reduce dose
- Avoid in cases of active GI bleeding
- Discontinue 2 weeks before surgery

## Drug Interactions
- Antidiabetic medications — may enhance hypoglycemic effect; monitor blood glucose
- Anticoagulants/antiplatelets — possible additive bleeding risk
- Iron supplements — tannins reduce iron absorption; separate dosing by 2 hours
- Digoxin — may affect absorption due to GI motility changes
- Oral medications in general — high tannin content may reduce absorption of some drugs
