# Musta (Cyperus rotundus)

## Profile
- **Category:** Digestive, Carminative, Anti-inflammatory
- **Ayurvedic:** Bitter-astringent-pungent (tikta-kashaya-katu) rasa, cooling (shita) virya, pungent (katu) vipaka; pacifies Pitta and Kapha, reduces Vata when processed with oils.

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Moderate
- **Key studies:**
  - Zhu M et al., Planta Medica, 1997 — alpha-cyperone and other sesquiterpenes from Cyperus rotundus showed anti-inflammatory activity via COX-2 inhibition.
  - Kilani S et al., Journal of Ethnopharmacology, 2005 — antioxidant and antimutagenic properties of C. rotundus tubers demonstrated in vitro.
  - Rastogi S et al., Journal of Traditional and Complementary Medicine, 2014 — clinical study confirmed efficacy of Musta in functional dyspepsia and IBS with significant symptom reduction.

## Key Compounds
- Alpha-cyperone
- Cyperene
- Patchoulenone
- Rotundone
- Mustakone

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 300-500 mg of standardized tuber extract, 2-3 times daily; 1-3 g of dried tuber powder.
- **Form:** Powder (churna), capsule, decoction (kwath), medicated oil, or as part of digestive formulas (Hingvastaka, Shankh Vati).
- **Duration:** Digestive benefits within 1-2 weeks; anti-inflammatory and GI healing effects at 4-8 weeks. Suitable for moderate-duration use of 2-3 months with periodic reassessment.

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Avoid in large doses during pregnancy — stimulates uterine contractions; small culinary doses are generally considered safe.
- Use cautiously in very dry constitutions or chronic constipation — astringent quality may worsen condition.
- Discontinue if acid reflux worsens despite initial improvement (rebound hyperacidity possible).
- Caution in patients with hormone-sensitive conditions — mild estrogenic activity reported in some studies.
- Not recommended for infants or very young children without practitioner guidance.

## Drug Interactions
- Antidiabetic medications — may enhance blood sugar lowering; monitor glucose levels.
- Sedatives and CNS depressants — mild calming effect may potentiate sedation.
- Hormonal therapies (HRT, oral contraceptives) — theoretical interaction via estrogenic compounds; monitor for reduced efficacy.
- Antacids and PPIs — may be complementary but timing separation (1-2 hours) recommended.
- Anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) — may allow dose reduction; consult prescriber.
