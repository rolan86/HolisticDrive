# Slippery Elm (Ulmus rubra)

## Profile
- **Category:** Gut herb / Demulcent
- **Ayurvedic:** Sweet, astringent rasa; cool virya; sweet vipaka; pacifies Pitta and Vata, may increase Kapha

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Low
- **Key studies:** O'Mathuna DP. "Evidence-based practice and herbal medicine." Nursing Clinics of North America, 2001 (demulcent review); Langmead L et al., Alimentary Pharmacology & Therapeutics, 2002 (mucosal protective herbs in GI disease); Babb JR et al., Journal of Hospital Infection, 1994 (barrier properties of mucilage)

## Key Compounds
- Mucilage (polysaccharides) — primary soothing demulcent agent
- Galactorhamnan — viscous fiber component
- Starch — contributes to emollient properties
- Tannins — mild astringent, anti-inflammatory
- Phytosterols — anti-inflammatory

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 400–500 mg of inner bark powder, 3 times daily; or 4 g powdered bark mixed in water for a soothing gruel
- **Form:** Lozenge, capsule, powder (mixed into water or porridge), tea (decoction of powdered bark)
- **Duration:** Soothing effects are typically immediate to same-day; safe for short-term and intermittent use; limited long-term safety data

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Take at least 2 hours apart from other oral medications — mucilage can significantly reduce drug absorption
- Avoid in intestinal obstruction or bowel strictures due to bulk-forming properties
- Use with caution in diabetic patients — may affect blood glucose absorption
- Pregnancy and lactation: generally regarded as safe when used in food amounts; supplement doses lack sufficient data
- Do not use if allergic to elm pollen

## Drug Interactions
- All oral medications — mucilage coats the GI tract and may delay or reduce absorption of concomitantly administered drugs
- Iron supplements — reduced absorption possible
- Diabetes medications — may alter glycemic response through slowed glucose absorption
- Lithium — possible decreased absorption

## Key Sources

- O'Mathuna DP. "Evidence-based practice and reviews of therapeutic touch." Journal of Nursing Scholarship. 2001.
- Ulbricht C et al. "Slippery elm (Ulmus rubra): an evidence-based systematic review." Journal of Herbal Pharmacotherapy. 2003.
