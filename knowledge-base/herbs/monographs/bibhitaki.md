# Bibhitaki (Terminalia bellirica)

## Profile
- **Category:** Rasayana, Rejuvenative, Digestive
- **Ayurvedic:** Astringent (kashaya) rasa, heating (ushna) virya, pungent (katu) vipaka; balances Kapha and Pitta, aggravates Vata in excess.

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Moderate
- **Key studies:**
  - Antony B et al., Journal of Ethnopharmacology, 2016 — anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activity of Terminalia bellirica fruit extract.
  - Saha S et al., Phytomedicine, 2018 — gallic acid derivatives from T. bellirica showed significant hepatoprotective effects in rodent models.
  - Patel S et al., BMC Complementary Medicine and Therapies, 2020 — clinical evaluation of Bibhitaki in functional constipation showed improvement over 4 weeks.

## Key Compounds
- Gallic acid
- Ellagic acid
- Tannins (chebulagic acid, chebulinic acid)
- Beta-sitosterol
- Glycosides (bellericanin)

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 250-500 mg of standardized extract, 2 times daily; 3-6 g of powdered fruit.
- **Form:** Powder (churna), capsule, decoction, or as part of Triphala blends.
- **Duration:** Digestive benefits noted within 1-2 weeks; rejuvenative effects develop over 4-12 weeks of consistent use. Safe for long-term use as a rasayana.

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Avoid in pregnancy due to astringent and downward-moving qualities.
- Use cautiously in individuals with severe dryness, dehydration, or chronic Vata aggravation.
- May cause mild gastric discomfort at high doses; start with lower doses and titrate.
- Discontinue 2 weeks before scheduled surgery due to potential effects on blood sugar.

## Drug Interactions
- Antidiabetic medications — may potentiate hypoglycemic effects; monitor blood glucose closely.
- Antihypertensive agents — may enhance blood pressure lowering; dose adjustment may be needed.
- Iron supplements — tannins may reduce iron absorption; separate administration by 2 hours.
- Immunomodulators — theoretical additive effect; monitor if used alongside immunosuppressants.
