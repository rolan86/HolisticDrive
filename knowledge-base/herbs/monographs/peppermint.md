# Peppermint (Mentha piperita)

## Profile
- **Category:** Gut herb / Carminative / Antispasmodic
- **Ayurvedic:** Pungent, sweet rasa; cool virya; pungent vipaka; pacifies Pitta and Kapha, may aggravate Vata in excess

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Strong (for IBS)
- **Key studies:** Ford AC et al., BMJ, 2008 (meta-analysis of peppermint oil in IBS — significant benefit); Khanna R et al., Journal of Clinical Gastroenterology, 2014 (enteric-coated peppermint oil for IBS); Grigoleit HG, Grigoleit P. "Peppermint oil in irritable bowel syndrome." Phytomedicine, 2005

## Key Compounds
- Menthol — primary active; antispasmodic, calcium-channel antagonist in GI smooth muscle
- Menthone — antispasmodic, analgesic
- Menthyl acetate — contributes to flavor and mild spasmolytic activity
- Flavonoids (luteolin, eriocitrin) — antioxidant, anti-inflammatory
- Rosmarinic acid — anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 180–225 mg enteric-coated peppermint oil capsules, 2–3 times daily (30 minutes before meals); or 1–2 cups of peppermint leaf tea as needed
- **Form:** Enteric-coated capsule (essential oil), tea (leaf), tincture, topical oil
- **Duration:** IBS symptom relief typically within 1–2 weeks; safe for up to 8–12 weeks at therapeutic doses; can be used intermittently long term

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Avoid in patients with GERD or hiatal hernia — peppermint can worsen reflux by relaxing the lower esophageal sphincter
- Avoid in patients with achlorhydria or active peptic ulcer disease
- Do not apply peppermint oil to the face of infants or young children — risk of bronchospasm and apnea
- Use enteric-coated capsules to avoid heartburn and dyspepsia
- Pregnancy: likely safe in culinary amounts; therapeutic doses should be supervised
- May worsen bile duct obstruction or gallbladder inflammation

## Drug Interactions
- Cyclosporine — peppermint oil may increase absorption and serum levels
- Antacids and H2 blockers — may dissolve enteric coating prematurely, reducing efficacy and causing heartburn
- CYP3A4 substrates — menthol may mildly inhibit metabolism
- Iron supplements — tannins in tea may reduce iron absorption
- Topical menthol may enhance transdermal drug absorption
