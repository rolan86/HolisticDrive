# Neem (Azadirachta indica)

## Profile
- **Category:** Antimicrobial, Blood Purifier, Anti-inflammatory
- **Ayurvedic:** Bitter (tikta) rasa, cooling (shita) virya, pungent (katu) vipaka; pacifies Pitta and Kapha, may aggravate Vata.

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Strong
- **Key studies:**
  - Biswas K et al., Indian Journal of Experimental Biology, 2002 — comprehensive review of neem's broad-spectrum antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties.
  - Subapriya R & Nagini S., Current Medicinal Chemistry, 2005 — chemopreventive and anticancer potential of neem limonoids across in vitro and in vivo models.
  - Alzohairy M.A., Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine, 2016 — review of therapeutic potential of Azadirachta indica with 67 referenced studies.

## Key Compounds
- Azadirachtin
- Nimbolide
- Nimbidin
- Gedunin
- Quercetin

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 300-600 mg of standardized leaf extract daily; 2-4 g of dried leaf powder.
- **Form:** Capsule, tablet, dried leaf powder, tea, topical oil, and decoction for external use.
- **Duration:** Skin and antimicrobial effects within 1-2 weeks; blood sugar modulation may take 4-8 weeks. Not recommended for continuous internal use beyond 8-12 weeks without supervision.

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Strictly contraindicated in pregnancy — may cause abortifacient effects; traditional texts explicitly warn against use.
- Avoid in children under 12 for internal use without practitioner guidance.
- Caution in individuals with severe Vata aggravation, emaciation, or low body weight.
- High doses may cause nausea, vomiting, or liver enzyme elevation; monitor liver function with prolonged use.
- Reduce dose or discontinue if experiencing excessive dryness of skin, mouth, or GI tract.

## Drug Interactions
- Antidiabetic medications — significant risk of additive hypoglycemia; close blood glucose monitoring required.
- Immunosuppressants — neem's immunostimulant action may counteract immunosuppressive therapy.
- Lithium — neem may have diuretic properties that alter lithium levels; avoid combination.
- Cytochrome P450 substrates — nimbolide may inhibit CYP3A4 and CYP2D6 metabolism.
