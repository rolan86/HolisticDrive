# Reishi (Ganoderma lucidum)

## Profile
- **Category:** Adaptogen / Immunomodulator
- **Ayurvedic:** Sweet, bitter rasa; neutral to warm virya; sweet vipaka; pacifies Kapha and Vata, may aggravate Pitta in excess

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Moderate
- **Key studies:** Gao Y et al., Journal of Ethnopharmacology, 2003 (immune modulation in cancer patients); Wachtel-Galor S et al., Herbal Medicine: Biomolecular and Clinical Aspects, 2011 (comprehensive review of bioactivity); Wang J et al., International Journal of Molecular Sciences, 2021 (beta-glucan and triterpenoid immunomodulatory mechanisms)

## Key Compounds
- Beta-glucans (polysaccharides) — primary immunomodulators
- Ganoderic acids (triterpenoids) — anti-inflammatory, hepatoprotective
- Lingzhi-8 protein — immune-regulating peptide
- Adenosine — cardiovascular support
- Ergosterol — precursor to vitamin D2

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 1,500–3,000 mg of dried fruiting body extract daily, divided into 2–3 doses
- **Form:** Dual-extracted capsule, tincture, or decoction (dual extraction recommended to capture both water-soluble polysaccharides and alcohol-soluble triterpenes)
- **Duration:** Immune effects may be noticed within 2–4 weeks; safe for long-term daily use as a tonic

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Avoid in individuals with known allergies to mushrooms or fungal spores
- Use with caution in patients on anticoagulant or antiplatelet therapy due to potential additive bleeding risk
- May lower blood pressure — monitor in patients on antihypertensives
- Discontinue 2 weeks before scheduled surgeries due to bleeding risk
- Pregnancy and lactation: insufficient safety data; avoid unless supervised by a clinician

## Drug Interactions
- Anticoagulants/antiplatelets (warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel) — may increase bleeding risk
- Antihypertensives — possible additive hypotensive effect
- Immunosuppressants — theoretical antagonism due to immunostimulant properties
- Chemotherapy agents — may interact; coordinate with oncologist
