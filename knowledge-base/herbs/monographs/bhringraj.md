# Bhringraj (Eclipta alba)

## Profile
- **Category:** Hair Tonic, Hepatoprotective, Rasayana
- **Ayurvedic:** Bitter-astringent (tikta-kashaya) rasa, cooling (shita) virya, pungent (katu) vipaka; pacifies Pitta and Kapha, reduces Vata when used with oil.

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Moderate
- **Key studies:**
  - Singh B et al., Phytomedicine, 2003 — significant hepatoprotective activity of Eclipta alba against chemically induced liver damage in rats.
  - Roy R.K. et al., Journal of Ethnopharmacology, 2008 — hair growth promoting activity of Eclipta alba extract comparable to minoxidil in mouse model.
  - Lakshmi B.V.S. et al., Drug Invention Today, 2014 — review of phytochemistry and pharmacology of Eclipta alba with 85 cited references.

## Key Compounds
- Wedelolactone
- Ecliptasaponin I-V
- Apigenin
- Luteolin
- Stigmasterol

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 250-500 mg of standardized extract, 2 times daily; 1-3 g of dried leaf powder.
- **Form:** Powder, capsule, medicated oil (Bhringraj Taila for topical hair application), decoction.
- **Duration:** Hair growth effects visible at 8-12 weeks of consistent topical and internal use. Hepatoprotective benefits noted within 4-6 weeks.

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Avoid during pregnancy and lactation without practitioner supervision.
- Use cautiously in individuals with very low blood pressure — mild hypotensive effects reported.
- May cause mild digestive upset at higher doses; administer with meals.
- Caution in bile duct obstruction — choleretic action may be inappropriate.
- Discontinue if skin irritation occurs with topical oil application.

## Drug Interactions
- Antihypertensive medications — may potentiate blood pressure lowering; monitor for orthostatic hypotension.
- Sedatives and CNS depressants — mild sedative action may enhance effects; caution with concurrent use.
- Antidiabetic drugs — may lower blood sugar; monitor glucose levels.
- Hepatotoxic drugs — protective effect may alter drug metabolism; timing separation advised.
