# Triphala (Terminalia chebula + T. bellirica + Emblica officinalis)

## Profile
- **Category:** Gut herb / Rasayana (rejuvenative)
- **Ayurvedic:** Mixed rasa (astringent, sweet, sour, bitter, pungent); warm virya; sweet vipaka; tridoshic — balances all three doshas

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Moderate
- **Key studies:** Peterson CT et al., Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine, 2017 (comprehensive review of Triphala); Sharma P et al., Journal of Ethnopharmacology, 2017 (antimicrobial and gut health); Chauhan B et al., International Journal of Pharmaceutical Sciences, 2017 (clinical therapeutics review)

## Key Compounds
- Chebulagic acid (T. chebula) — antioxidant, anti-inflammatory
- Gallic acid (all three fruits) — antimicrobial, antioxidant
- Ellagic acid (E. officinalis, T. chebula) — anti-cancer, hepatoprotective
- Emblicanin A & B (E. officinalis) — potent vitamin C antioxidants
- Chebulinic acid (T. chebula) — laxative, antimicrobial

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 500–1,000 mg of standardized extract, 1–2 times daily; or 3–5 g of churna (powder) in warm water before bed
- **Form:** Churna (powder), capsule, tablet, decoction
- **Duration:** Digestive regulation within 1–2 weeks; rejuvenative effects are cumulative over months; safe for long-term daily use per Ayurvedic tradition

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Avoid in acute diarrhea — Triphala has laxative properties
- Use with caution in patients with active peptic ulcer (the astringent component may be irritating)
- Pregnancy: avoid in therapeutic doses — mild uterine stimulant properties reported in some studies
- May cause loose stools in sensitive individuals — reduce dose if needed
- Discontinue before and after surgery due to potential blood sugar effects

## Drug Interactions
- Antidiabetic medications — may enhance hypoglycemic effect (improves insulin sensitivity)
- Anticoagulants — possible additive effect due to antiplatelet activity of gallic acid
- Iron supplements — tannins may reduce iron absorption; separate dosing by 2 hours
- Immunosuppressants — immunomodulatory effects may theoretically antagonize
