# Chitrak (Plumbago zeylanica)

## Profile
- **Category:** Digestive Stimulant, Anti-inflammatory, Kapha Reducer
- **Ayurvedic:** Pungent-bitter (katu-tikta) rasa, heating (ushna) virya, pungent (katu) vipaka; pacifies Kapha and Vata, aggravates Pitta strongly.

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Moderate
- **Key studies:**
  - Bhatt J.B. et al., Indian Journal of Pharmacology, 1988 — anti-inflammatory and analgesic properties of Plumbago zeylanica root validated in animal models.
  - Tewari D et al., Frontiers in Pharmacology, 2018 — comprehensive review of Plumbago species covering pharmacology, phytochemistry, and traditional uses.
  - Ye M et al., Journal of Ethnopharmacology, 2005 — plumbagin from P. zeylanica demonstrated significant anticancer activity in multiple cell lines.

## Key Compounds
- Plumbagin
- Plumbazin
- Droserone
- Elliptone
- Zeylanone

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 125-250 mg of purified root extract, 1-2 times daily; 250-500 mg of root powder (after proper shodhana/purification).
- **Form:** Purified powder (shodhit churna), capsule, decoction (kwath), medicated ghee (Chitrakadi Ghrita).
- **Duration:** Digestive stimulation within days; anti-inflammatory effects at 2-4 weeks. Short courses of 2-4 weeks recommended due to potency. Extended use only under practitioner guidance.

## Cautions & Contraindications
- Contraindicated in pregnancy — plumbagin has abortifacient and teratogenic potential in animal studies.
- Avoid in breastfeeding women.
- Strictly avoid in Pitta-dominant conditions (ulcers, gastritis, bleeding disorders, inflammatory skin conditions).
- Must be used only after proper Ayurvedic purification (shodhana) — raw root is significantly more toxic.
- Caution in patients with kidney disease — nephrotoxicity reported at high doses.
- Not suitable for children, elderly, or debilitated individuals.

## Drug Interactions
- Anticoagulants (warfarin, heparin) — plumbagin inhibits platelet aggregation; increased bleeding risk.
- Antidiabetic medications — may potentiate hypoglycemia; close monitoring required.
- Chemotherapeutic agents — may have additive or synergistic cytotoxic effects; requires oncologist oversight.
- NSAIDs — combined use increases GI irritation and ulcer risk.
- Cytochrome P450 substrates — plumbagin is a potent CYP inhibitor; may significantly alter drug levels.
