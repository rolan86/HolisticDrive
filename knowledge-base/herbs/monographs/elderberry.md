# Elderberry (Sambucus nigra)

## Profile
- **Category:** Immunomodulator, Antiviral, Antioxidant, Anti-inflammatory
- **Ayurvedic:** Bitter, sweet, astringent rasa; cooling virya; sweet vipaka; pacifies Pitta and Kapha, mildly aggravates Vata

## Evidence
- **Evidence level:** Moderate
- **Key studies:** Hawkins et al., Nutrients, 2019 (RCT, cold duration reduction); Zakay-Rones et al., J Int Med Res, 2004 (RCT, influenza); Vlachojannis et al., Phytother Res, 2010 (systematic review)

## Key Compounds
- Anthocyanins (cyanidin-3-glucoside, cyanidin-3-sambubioside)
- Flavonoids (quercetin, rutin)
- Phenolic acids (chlorogenic acid, caffeic acid)
- Lectins (removed in properly prepared extracts)
- Tannins

## Dosing
- **Typical dose:** 500-1500 mg standardized elderberry extract, 2-3 times daily
- **Form:** Capsule, syrup, gummy, lozenge, tea (dried berries only — never raw)
- **Duration:** Best used at onset of viral symptoms for 3-5 days; prophylactic use during cold/flu season (up to 12 weeks); safe for seasonal use with breaks

## Cautions & Contraindications
- **NEVER consume raw elderberries** — contain cyanogenic glycosides (sambunigrin) causing nausea, vomiting, and toxicity
- Use cautiously in autoimmune conditions (immune-stimulating properties)
- Avoid in pregnancy and lactation (insufficient safety data)
- Caution in diabetes (high sugar content in syrups — use sugar-free extracts)
- Only use properly prepared, commercially processed extracts

## Drug Interactions
- Immunosuppressants (corticosteroids, biologics) — may reduce efficacy due to immune stimulation
- Antidiabetic medications — may enhance blood sugar lowering effects
- Diuretics — may have additive diuretic effects
- Laxatives — concurrent use may increase GI motility
- Chemotherapy drugs — theoretical interaction, consult oncologist
