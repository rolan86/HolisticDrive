## Key Principles
- **Daytime vs. nighttime** — stimulating nervines (Brahmi, Holy Basil) for day; sedating (Valerian, Tagara) for evening
- **Onset of action varies** — Passionflower/Valerian work in hours; Brahmi/Holy Basil take weeks
- **Combination strategies** — adaptogenic nervines + sedative nervines for comprehensive support
- **Cycling is important** — rotate nervines every 4-6 weeks to prevent tolerance
- **Quality sleep hygiene** — nervines work best with consistent sleep schedule

## Cautions
- Sedative nervines impair driving/alertness — use caution until effects are known
- May interact with benzodiazepines, barbiturates, and alcohol — additive CNS depression
- Not all nervines are safe in pregnancy — Passionflower, Valerian contraindicated
- Some can cause paradoxical stimulation — especially Valerian in sensitive individuals
- Long-term safety data limited for some nervines — periodic reassessment recommended
- May interact with antidepressants and antipsychotics — professional guidance advised
