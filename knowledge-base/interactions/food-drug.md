# Food-Drug Interactions Database

| Food / Beverage | Drug Class | Specific Drugs | Mechanism | Clinical Effect | Recommendation |
|-----------------|------------|----------------|-----------|-----------------|----------------|
| Grapefruit / Grapefruit juice | Statins (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors) | Simvastatin, lovastatin, atorvastatin | Inhibits intestinal CYP3A4 and OATP1B1 transporters, increasing drug bioavailability | Increased statin blood levels; risk of rhabdomyolysis, myopathy, elevated CK | Avoid grapefruit entirely with simvastatin/lovastatin; limit with atorvastatin; pravastatin/rosuvastatin are safe alternatives |
| Grapefruit / Grapefruit juice | Calcium channel blockers (CCBs) | Felodipine, nifedipine, verapamil | Inhibits intestinal CYP3A4 | Increased drug levels; exaggerated hypotension, tachycardia, edema | Avoid grapefruit; switch to non-CYP3A4 metabolized CCB if needed |
| Grapefruit / Grapefruit juice | Immunosuppressants | Cyclosporine, tacrolimus | CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein inhibition in gut | Increased drug levels; nephrotoxicity, tremor, hypertension | Avoid grapefruit; maintain consistent diet; monitor drug levels closely |
| Grapefruit / Grapefruit juice | Certain benzodiazepines | Midazolam, triazolam, diazepam | CYP3A4 inhibition increases oral bioavailability | Enhanced and prolonged sedation | Avoid grapefruit; lorazepam and oxazepam are safer alternatives |
| Vitamin K-rich foods (kale, spinach, broccoli, Brussels sprouts) | Anticoagulants | Warfarin | Vitamin K is essential cofactor for clotting factors II, VII, IX, X; opposes warfarin's vitamin K epoxide reductase inhibition | Decreased INR; reduced anticoagulant effect; increased thrombotic risk | Maintain consistent daily vitamin K intake; do not eliminate or drastically increase; dietary changes require INR monitoring |
| Tyramine-rich foods (aged cheese, cured meats, fermented foods, red wine, soy sauce, draft beer) | MAOIs (monoamine oxidase inhibitors) | Phenelzine, tranylcypromine, selegiline, isocarboxazid | MAO inhibition prevents tyramine breakdown; accumulated tyramine triggers norepinephrine release | Hypertensive crisis: severe headache, hypertension, intracranial hemorrhage, death | Strict tyramine-restricted diet (<50mg/day); educate patient thoroughly; avoid aged/fermented/pickled foods |
| Calcium supplements / Calcium-fortified foods | Thyroid hormone | Levothyroxine | Calcium binds levothyroxine in GI tract forming insoluble complexes | Reduced levothyroxine absorption; elevated TSH; hypothyroid symptoms | Separate levothyroxine and calcium by at least 4 hours; take levothyroxine on empty stomach, 30-60 min before breakfast |
| Iron supplements | Thyroid hormone | Levothyroxine | Iron binds levothyroxine forming insoluble complexes in GI tract | Reduced levothyroxine absorption; inadequately controlled hypothyroidism | Separate by at least 4 hours; take levothyroxine first thing in morning |
| Iron supplements | Tetracyclines | Tetracycline, doxycycline, minocycline | Iron chelates tetracycline in GI tract forming insoluble complexes | Reduced antibiotic absorption; treatment failure | Separate by at least 2-3 hours; take antibiotic on empty stomach or 1 hr before/2 hr after meals |
| Iron supplements | Fluoroquinolones | Ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, moxifloxacin | Iron chelates fluoroquinolone in GI tract | Reduced antibiotic absorption and efficacy | Separate by at least 2-4 hours; check specific drug labeling |
| Dairy products (milk, yogurt, cheese) | Tetracyclines | Tetracycline, doxycycline | Calcium in dairy chelates tetracycline molecules | Reduced absorption; treatment failure | Take tetracycline 1 hour before or 2 hours after dairy; avoid dairy within 2 hours of dosing |
| Dairy products (milk, yogurt, cheese) | Fluoroquinolones | Ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin | Calcium cations chelate fluoroquinolone, reducing absorption | Decreased antibiotic efficacy | Separate dairy from fluoroquinolone by at least 2 hours; check specific product instructions |
| High-fat meals | Lipophilic drugs (increased absorption) | Griseofulvin, albendazole, lumefantrine, tipranavir, isotretinoin | Fat stimulates bile release and improves solubilization of lipophilic drugs | Increased drug absorption (sometimes desired); enhanced efficacy or toxicity | Take griseofulvin with fatty meal (required); check specific drug -- some require high-fat meal for absorption |
| High-fat meals | Drugs with delayed absorption | Propranolol, itraconazole, saquinavir, carvedilol | Fat slows gastric emptying, delaying drug dissolution and absorption | Delayed Tmax; potentially reduced Cmax; may affect efficacy timing | Follow drug-specific instructions; some require food, others fasting |
| Alcohol | Benzodiazepines / Barbiturates | Diazepam, lorazepam, alprazolam, phenobarbital | Additive CNS depression; both enhance GABA-A receptor activity | Excessive sedation, respiratory depression, coma, death | Avoid alcohol entirely; warn patients of compounded risk |
| Alcohol | Metronidazole | Metronidazole | Inhibits aldehyde dehydrogenase causing acetaldehyde accumulation (disulfiram-like reaction) | Nausea, vomiting, flushing, headache, tachycardia, palpitations | Avoid alcohol during treatment and 72 hours after stopping metronidazole |
| Alcohol | Acetaminophen (paracetamol) | Acetaminophen | Alcohol induces CYP2E1, increasing conversion to NAPQI (hepatotoxic metabolite); also depletes glutathione | Increased hepatotoxicity risk; liver failure at lower acetaminophen doses | Limit acetaminophen to 2-3g/day if alcohol use; avoid chronic alcohol + acetaminophen; educate on cumulative risk |
| Alcohol | SSRIs / SNRIs | Fluoxetine, sertraline, venlafaxine, citalopram | Additive CNS depression; alcohol worsens depression/anxiety counteracting drug benefit | Increased sedation, impaired judgment, worsened mood | Limit alcohol; advise patients that even moderate drinking may reduce medication effectiveness |
| Alcohol | Opioids | Codeine, morphine, oxycodone, fentanyl | Additive CNS and respiratory depression | Profound sedation, respiratory arrest, death | Avoid alcohol entirely; educate on life-threatening risk |
| Potassium-rich foods (bananas, oranges, potatoes, tomatoes, avocados, spinach, salt substitutes) | ACE inhibitors | Lisinopril, enalapril, ramipril | ACE inhibitors reduce aldosterone, decreasing renal potassium excretion | Hyperkalemia; muscle weakness, arrhythmias, cardiac arrest | Monitor serum potassium; avoid salt substitutes (high in KCl); do not supplement potassium without medical supervision |
| Potassium-rich foods | Potassium-sparing diuretics | Spironolactone, eplerenone, amiloride, triamterene | Drug reduces renal potassium excretion; high intake compounds effect | Hyperkalemia; cardiac arrhythmias | Monitor potassium levels; limit high-potassium foods; avoid salt substitutes |
| Leafy greens (kale, spinach, collard greens, Swiss chard, Brussels sprouts) | Warfarin | Warfarin | Dietary vitamin K counteracts warfarin's mechanism of vitamin K epoxide reductase inhibition | Fluctuating INR; risk of clotting (if INR drops) or bleeding (if INR rises from sudden change) | Maintain consistent daily intake; sudden dietary changes require more frequent INR monitoring |
| Charcoal-grilled / smoked foods | Methylxanthines | Theophylline, caffeine | Charcoal grilling induces CYP1A2, accelerating theophylline metabolism | Decreased theophylline levels; reduced bronchodilator effect | Maintain consistent diet; avoid sudden increase in charcoal-grilled foods; monitor theophylline levels |
| High-fiber foods (bran, oats, psyllium) | Multiple drug classes | Digoxin, levothyroxine, tricyclic antidepressants | Fiber binds drugs in GI tract reducing absorption | Reduced drug bioavailability; subtherapeutic levels | Separate high-fiber meals from medication by 2 hours where possible |
| Cranberry juice | Warfarin | Warfarin | Cranberry flavonoids may inhibit CYP2C9, reducing warfarin metabolism | Increased INR; elevated bleeding risk | Monitor INR if patient consumes cranberry juice regularly; avoid large quantities |
| Pomelo | CYP3A4 substrates | Same as grapefruit interactions | Pomelo also contains furanocoumarins that inhibit CYP3A4 | Similar to grapefruit: increased drug levels and toxicity | Avoid pomelo with CYP3A4 substrates; Seville oranges also carry risk |
| St. John's Wort (herb, consumed as tea/supplement) | Oral contraceptives, immunosuppressants, SSRIs, warfarin | Multiple | CYP3A4 and P-gp induction accelerates drug metabolism | Reduced drug efficacy; breakthrough bleeding, transplant rejection, pregnancy | Avoid St. John's Wort with critical medications; 2-week washout period needed |
