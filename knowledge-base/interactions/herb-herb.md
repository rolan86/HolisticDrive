## Antagonistic Combinations

| Herb A | Herb B | Type | Effect | Recommendation |
|--------|--------|------|--------|----------------|
| Caffeine-containing herbs (guarana, yerba mate, green tea) | Valerian | Antagonistic | CNS stimulation counteracts sedative effect; reduced efficacy of sleep aid | Separate by at least 6 hours; avoid evening caffeine |
| Caffeine-containing herbs (guarana, yerba mate, green tea) | Chamomile | Antagonistic | Mild stimulant vs. mild sedative; reduced calming effect | Separate timing; avoid concurrent use |
| Ephedra (ma huang) | Valerian or Passionflower | Antagonistic | Strong sympathomimetic stimulation directly opposes sedation | Do not combine; ephedra is restricted/banned in many countries |
| Ginseng | Valerian | Antagonistic | Ginseng's stimulating properties counteract valerian's sedation | Avoid concurrent use for sleep; may use ginseng AM, valerian PM with 8+ hr gap |
| Green tea | Ashwagandha | Antagonistic | Caffeine-induced anxiety may counteract ashwagandha's anxiolytic benefit | Take ashwagandha without caffeine or at separate times |
| Licorice | Spironolactone | Antagonistic | Licorice's mineralocorticoid effect opposes spironolactone's potassium-sparing diuretic action | Avoid combination; risk of hypokalemia or hyperkalemia |

## Contraindicated Combinations

| Herb A | Herb B | Type | Effect | Recommendation |
|--------|--------|------|--------|----------------|
| Licorice | Warfarin / Blood thinners | Contraindicated | Licorice depletes potassium and potentiates bleeding; warfarin's anticoagulant effect may be enhanced unpredictably | Avoid concurrent use entirely; licorice also raises BP which compounds cardiovascular risk |
| Echinacea | Immunosuppressants (cyclosporine, tacrolimus, prednisone) | Contraindicated | Immune stimulation directly counteracts immunosuppressive therapy; risk of organ rejection in transplant patients | Strictly avoid; may reduce drug levels via CYP interaction |
| Astragalus | Immunosuppressants | Contraindicated | Astragalus stimulates immune function, opposing immunosuppressive drug action | Avoid in transplant patients and those on immunosuppressive therapy |
| Ginkgo | Warfarin / Antiplatelet drugs | Contraindicated | Additive anticoagulant effect; significantly increased bleeding risk | Discontinue ginkgo 2 weeks before surgery or when on anticoagulants |
| Garlic (high-dose supplements) | Warfarin / Antiplatelet drugs | Contraindicated | Garlic's antiplatelet compounds (ajoene) potentiate bleeding risk | Avoid high-dose garlic supplements; dietary amounts generally safe |
| Ginseng | Warfarin | Contraindicated | Ginseng may decrease warfarin effectiveness (reduced INR), leading to thrombotic risk | Do not combine; monitor INR closely if patient uses ginseng |
| St. John's Wort | SSRI antidepressants | Contraindicated | Risk of serotonin syndrome; St. John's Wort also induces CYP450 reducing SSRI levels | Strictly avoid; allow 2-week washout when switching |
| St. John's Wort | Oral contraceptives | Contraindicated | CYP3A4 induction reduces contraceptive efficacy; risk of breakthrough bleeding and unintended pregnancy | Use alternative contraception; avoid combination |
| St. John's Wort | Immunosuppressants | Contraindicated | CYP3A4 induction dramatically lowers cyclosporine/tacrolimus levels; risk of organ rejection | Strictly avoid in transplant patients |
| Kava | Benzodiazepines or other sedatives | Contraindicated | Additive CNS depression; risk of profound sedation, respiratory depression; potential hepatotoxicity | Do not combine; both have independent hepatotoxicity concerns |
| Ginger (high-dose) | Anticoagulants | Contraindicated | Ginger inhibits platelet aggregation; additive bleeding risk with warfarin, heparin, NOACs | Avoid high-dose ginger supplements; culinary amounts generally acceptable |
| Dan Shen (Salvia) | Warfarin | Contraindicated | Potent additive anticoagulant effect; significantly increases INR and bleeding risk | Strictly avoid concurrent use |
