# Ritucharya: Seasonal Living Protocols

Ayurveda divides the year into two solstitial cycles (Adana and Visarga Kala), each containing three seasons. Diet and lifestyle must shift with each season to maintain dosha balance and prevent disease.

**Northern Hemisphere Month Correlations:**
- Hemanta (Late Winter): mid-January to mid-March
- Shishira (Winter): mid-November to mid-January
- Vasanta (Spring): mid-March to mid-May
- Grishma (Summer): mid-May to mid-July
- Varsha (Monsoon): mid-July to mid-September
- Sharad (Autumn): mid-September to mid-November

---

## 1. Hemanta (Late Winter) -- mid-January to mid-March

**Dominant Dosha:** Kapha accumulates; Agni is strong (Visarga Kala -- nature's nourishing phase)

### Dietary Guidelines
- This is the season for heavy, sweet, sour, and salty foods -- agni can handle them
- Favor: ghee, warm milk, butter, sweet rice preparations, wheat, new rice
- Warm, nourishing soups with root vegetables (sweet potato, carrots, beets)
- Include healthy fats: sesame oil, ghee, almond oil
- Sweet fruits: bananas, dates, figs, grapes
- Avoid: cold foods, raw salads, light fasting (digestion is too strong for deprivation)

### Lifestyle Adjustments
- Apply warm sesame oil (abhyanga) before bathing -- prevents Vata aggravation from cold
- Exercise vigorously; the body can handle intense physical activity
- Wear warm clothing, protect from wind and cold
- Exposure to morning sunlight
- Sexual activity can be more frequent (strength is high)

### Herbs
- Favor: Ashwagandha, Shatavari, Bala, Vidari -- nourishing and building
- Avoid: Laxatives and strong purgatives; fasting is counterproductive
- Cinnamon, ginger, and cardamom in warm milk before bed

### Transitioning Practice
- As Hemanta shifts to Shishira, maintain warmth but begin slightly lighter foods if Kapha symptoms appear (congestion, heaviness)

---

## 2. Shishira (Winter) -- mid-November to mid-January

**Dominant Dosha:** Kapha and Vata; cold and dry intensify (late Visarga into early Adana)

### Dietary Guidelines
- Continue heavy, nourishing foods but increase warming qualities
- Favor: warm, spiced milk with turmeric and ginger; hearty stews; hot cereals
- Fermented foods like idli, dosa, kanji are acceptable and supportive
- Include honey in moderation (never heated above 40C/104F -- becomes toxic per Ayurveda)
- Meat soups and bone broths if non-vegetarian
- Avoid: cold drinks, raw foods, excessive dry snacks

### Lifestyle Adjustments
- Continue warm sesame oil abhyanga daily
- Protect extremities from cold; cover head and ears outdoors
- Avoid exposure to cold wind (Vata aggravation)
- Light a fire or use heating pads on cold mornings
- Early to bed, early to rise (before 6 AM)

### Herbs
- Favor: Ginger (fresh and dry), Trikatu, Chitrak -- kindle agni against cold
- Dashamoola (ten-root formula) for joint and respiratory support
- Pippali (long pepper) with honey for respiratory immunity

### Transitioning Practice
- Late Shishira begins the Adana Kala (sun's strength increases, nature begins extracting)
- Start reducing very heavy foods as Vasanta approaches
- Begin incorporating more bitter greens (dandelion, kale)

---

## 3. Vasanta (Spring) -- mid-March to mid-May

**Dominant Dosha:** Kapha is aggravated and liquefied; allergies, congestion, sluggishness peak

### Dietary Guidelines
- Primary goal: reduce Kapha accumulated over winter
- Favor: light, dry, warm, pungent, bitter, astringent foods
- Barley, oats, millet (lighter grains)
- Honey (especially with warm water on waking -- scrapes Kapha)
- Lentils and legumes (moong dal, masoor dal)
- Bitter greens: dandelion, fenugreek, mustard greens
- Avoid: heavy dairy, sweets, fried foods, cold drinks, new grains
- Old grains (grains stored 1+ year) are preferred over fresh harvest

### Lifestyle Adjustments
- Vigorous exercise is essential -- Kapha must be moved
- Dry brushing (Garshana) with raw silk gloves before shower
- Kapha-reducing abhyanga with dry mustard powder or light sesame oil
- Avoid daytime sleeping (directly increases Kapha)
- Nasya (nasal oil therapy) with Anu taila or Triphala water -- clears sinuses
- Kavala (oil pulling) with sesame oil to clear congestion

### Herbs
- Favor: Trikatu, Punarnava, Guggulu, Turmeric -- Kapha-clearing
- Triphala churna daily for gentle detoxification
- Sitopaladi churna for respiratory congestion and allergies
- Neem for blood purification
- Avoid: Heavy, sweet, cooling herbs like Shatavari in excess

### Transitioning Practice
- As Vasanta ends, gradually reduce intense Kapha-reducing measures
- Begin increasing sweet and cooling foods to prepare for Pitta season

---

## 4. Grishma (Summer) -- mid-May to mid-July

**Dominant Dosha:** Vata accumulates (dryness from heat); Pitta is also high (sun's intensity, Adana Kala peak)

### Dietary Guidelines
- Primary goal: pacify Pitta, prevent Vata accumulation
- Favor: sweet, bitter, astringent tastes; cold or room-temperature foods
- Sweet fruits: grapes, melons, pomegranates, mangoes, coconut
- Light grains: rice, barley, oats
- Ghee as primary fat (cooling despite being oily)
- Lassi (yogurt drink diluted with water and cumin/coriander) -- excellent for summer
- Drink plenty of room-temperature or cool water infused with mint, rose, or fennel
- Avoid: pungent, sour, salty foods; alcohol; excessive sun exposure after eating
- Avoid yogurt plain (too sour/heating); always dilute as lassi

### Lifestyle Adjustments
- Apply cooling coconut oil or sandalwood oil for abhyanga
- Wear light, loose cotton clothing; cooling colors (white, blue, green)
- Exercise only during early morning or evening (before 8 AM or after 6 PM)
- Avoid direct midday sun; rest in cool, shaded areas
- Moonlight walks are recommended (cooling, calming)
- Swimming is the ideal exercise
- Sleep in a well-ventilated room; fan or air conditioning acceptable
- Apply sandalwood paste or rose water to forehead and temples

### Herbs
- Favor: Amalaki, Shatavari, Brahmi, Rose, Coriander -- cooling herbs
- Shatavari + Ghee + milk as a summer tonic
- Mint, fennel, coriander seeds as digestive spices
- Avoid: Heating herbs like dry ginger, Trikatu, Chitrak unless specifically needed

### Transitioning Practice
- As Grishma ends, Vata has accumulated significantly
- Begin transitioning to slightly heavier and warmer foods
- Increase ghee intake to prepare for Varsha (monsoon)

---

## 5. Varsha (Monsoon) -- mid-July to mid-September

**Dominant Dosha:** Vata is provoked (cold, damp, variable); Agni is weakened; Pitta may also aggravate

### Dietary Guidelines
- Primary goal: kindle weakened agni, pacify Vata
- Favor: sour, salty, and oily foods; warm, well-cooked meals
- Aged grains (wheat, rice stored from previous harvest)
- Soups with medicated ghee, light but warming
- Honey in warm (not hot) water
- Fermented foods (buttermilk, pickled vegetables) in moderation
- Include rock salt in cooking (heating and digestive)
- Avoid: raw salads, leafy greens (contamination risk in monsoon), river fish, excess milk
- Boil all water; add ginger or cumin to drinking water

### Lifestyle Adjustments
- Apply warm sesame oil abhyanga daily -- Vata is high despite humidity
- Avoid exposure to dampness; keep living spaces dry
- Use Panchakarma therapies if possible (ideal season for detox)
- Avoid daytime sleeping (weakens agni further)
- Avoid walking barefoot; fungal infections are common
- Fumigate rooms with herbs (guggulu dhoop) to purify air
- Wear warm, dry clothing even though it is not cold

### Herbs
- Favor: Haritaki (specifically -- called "Abhaya" in Varsha), Dashamoola, Ginger
- Haritaki with rock salt and warm water after meals
- Trikatu before meals to kindle agni
- Guduchi (Giloy) for immunity during seasonal illness
- Avoid: Heavy, sweet herbs; purgatives

### Transitioning Practice
- Late Varsha: begin reducing sour foods as Sharad approaches
- Pitta will rise as sun strengthens again -- start Pitta-pacifying measures early

---

## 6. Sharad (Autumn) -- mid-September to mid-November

**Dominant Dosha:** Pitta aggravation (sun regains strength, accumulated heat from summer flares); Adana Kala

### Dietary Guidelines
- Primary goal: pacify Pitta, prevent Pitta disorders (skin inflammation, fevers, digestive upset)
- Favor: sweet, bitter, astringent; ghee-enriched foods
- Ghee is the single most important food this season -- it pacifies Pitta and kindles agni
- Rice, green gram (moong dal), sugar, Amla (Indian gooseberry)
- Sweet fruits: pomegranate, grapes, coconut
- Cold water purified with sandalwood or musta (nutgrass) is traditional
- Avoid: hot, spicy, oily, fermented foods; yogurt; garlic; onion
- Avoid exposure to heat and direct sunlight after meals

### Lifestyle Adjustments
- Apply Chandana (sandalwood) paste or cool water to the body
- Wear pearls, moonstones; light-colored clothing
- Apply ghee to nostrils (Nasya) -- specific Pitta-reducing practice for Sharad
- Moonbathing in the early evening
- Avoid excessive exercise; moderate yoga and walking
- Fumigation with guggulu and sandalwood

### Herbs
- Favor: Amalaki (single best herb for Sharad), Guduchi, Shatavari
- Amlaki churna with honey -- powerful Pitta pacifier
- Coriander seed tea throughout the day
- Shatavari Ghrita (Shatavari in ghee) as medicine
- Avoid: Pungent, heating herbs

### Transitioning Practice
- Late Sharad transitions into Hemanta (nourishing phase)
- Begin increasing heavier foods and fats to build strength for winter
- Restart warm sesame oil abhyanga as temperatures drop

## Seasonal Transition Quick-Reference

| Season | Dominant Dosha | Key Taste Focus | Best Oil (Abhyanga) | Primary Herb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hemanta | Kapha (accumulates) | Sweet, sour, salty | Warm sesame | Ashwagandha |
| Shishira | Kapha + Vata | Sweet, sour, salty, warming | Warm sesame | Ginger/Pippali |
| Vasanta | Kapha (aggravated) | Pungent, bitter, astringent | Dry/mustard | Trikatu, Punarnava |
| Grishma | Pitta + Vata | Sweet, bitter, astringent | Coconut | Amalaki, Rose |
| Varsha | Vata (aggravated) | Sour, salty, warming | Warm sesame | Haritaki, Guduchi |
| Sharad | Pitta (aggravated) | Sweet, bitter, astringent | Coconut or cooling | Amalaki |
