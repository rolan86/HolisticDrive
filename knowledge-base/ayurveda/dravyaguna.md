# Dravyaguna: Ayurvedic Herbal Pharmacology

Dravyaguna (science of medicinal substances) classifies herbs and foods by their energetic properties. Understanding these categories allows prediction of an herb's effect on the body and mind, and how it interacts with specific doshas.

---

## Core Concepts

### Rasa (Taste) -- Immediate Effect
The six tastes (Shadrasa) are perceived on the tongue and indicate the herb's immediate action. Rasa influences the first stage of digestion and directly affects doshas.

- **Madhura (Sweet):** Nourishing, building, cooling; pacifies Vata and Pitta
- **Amla (Sour):** Stimulating, digestive; pacifies Vata; aggravates Pitta and Kapha
- **Lavana (Salty):** Softening, hydrating; pacifies Vata; aggravates Pitta and Kapha
- **Katu (Pungent):** Stimulating, heating; pacifies Kapha; aggravates Vata and Pitta
- **Tikta (Bitter):** Detoxifying, cooling; pacifies Pitta and Kapha; aggravates Vata
- **Kashaya (Astringent):** Drying, tightening; pacifies Pitta and Kapha; aggravates Vata

### Virya (Potency) -- Action During Digestion
The heating or cooling energy of a substance, experienced during digestion.

- **Ushna (Heating):** Stimulates agni, promotes circulation, induces sweating, reduces Kapha and Vata (in excess). Examples: ginger, black pepper, mustard.
- **Sheeta (Cooling):** Soothes inflammation, calms Pitta, nourishes tissues, can slow digestion. Examples: amalaki, mint, coriander.

### Vipaka (Post-Digestive Effect) -- Long-Term Effect
The taste that manifests after complete digestion, indicating the herb's ultimate metabolic impact.

- **Madhura Vipaka (Sweet post-digestive effect):** Nourishes, builds, strengthens; caused by sweet and salty rasa. Long-term building action.
- **Amla Vipaka (Sour post-digestive effect):** Increases heat and pitta; caused by sour rasa. Long-term stimulating action.
- **Katu Vipaka (Pungent post-digestive effect):** Cleanses, dries, reduces Kapha; caused by pungent, bitter, and astringent rasa. Long-term reducing action.

### Prabhava (Unique/Unexplainable Action)
The specific, often unpredictable effect of a substance that cannot be deduced from rasa, virya, or vipaka alone. It is the "special power" of the herb.

- Example: Amalaki is sweet, sour, astringent, cooling, and sweet post-digestive -- yet it is one of the most powerful digestive and agni-kindling herbs. This unique capacity is its prabhava.
- Example: Garlic has a heating virya yet is used as an aphrodisiac (typically a sweet/heating combination) -- its specific aphrodisiac action beyond its predicted effect is prabhava.

### How to Use These Concepts
1. **Match the herb to the imbalance:** Kapha aggravation needs Katu/Tikta rasa, Ushna virya, Katu vipaka. Pitta aggravation needs Madhura/Tikta rasa, Sheeta virya, Madhura vipaka.
2. **Consider the full profile:** A herb may pacify one dosha while aggravating another. Ginger (Katu rasa, Ushna virya) pacifies Kapha but aggravates Pitta in excess.
3. **Account for Vipaka for long-term use:** Herbs with Katu vipaka are reducing and best for short-term cleansing. Madhura vipaka herbs are building and suitable for long-term nourishment.
4. **Prabhava overrides theory:** When clinical experience shows a herb working differently than its rasa/virya/vipaka predict, trust the prabhava.

## Triphala: The Most Important Ayurvedic Formula

Triphala ("three fruits") is the most widely prescribed Ayurvedic formulation. It is unique in being both a detoxifying agent and a rejuvenative (rasayana).

### Composition
- **Amalaki (1 part):** Nourishing, cooling, Pitta-pacifying, rich in vitamin C
- **Bibhitaki (1 part):** Kapha-reducing, expectorant, astringent
- **Haritaki (1 part):** Vata-pacifying, laxative, rejuvenates all tissues

### Dosage
- **Powder (churna):** 1/2 to 1 tsp in warm water before bed (constipation) or upon waking (general health)
- **Tablets:** 1-2 tablets twice daily with warm water
- **Decoction:** Boil 1 tsp in 2 cups water until reduced to 1 cup; strain and drink
- **Eye wash:** Triphala water (strained thoroughly through fine cloth) for eye inflammation

### Key Properties
- Tridoshic (balances all three doshas)
- Contains five of the six tastes (all except salty)
- Gentle laxative that does not create dependence
- Rich in antioxidants; anti-aging
- Supports all seven dhatus (tissues)
- Safe for long-term daily use

## Practical Guidelines for Herbal Use

### Forms of Administration
- **Churna (powder):** Most common; taken with warm water, honey, ghee, or milk depending on dosha
- **Kwatha (decoction):** For roots, bark, and hard parts; boil 1 part herb in 16 parts water until reduced to 1/4
- **Ghrita (medicated ghee):** For nourishing and Pitta-pacifying preparations
- **Taila (medicated oil):** For external application (abhyanga, nasya) or internal use
- **Asava/Arishta (fermented liquid):** For digestive and respiratory conditions; self-preserved
- **Avaleha (jam/paste):** For rejuvenative preparations (e.g., Chyawanprash)

### Anupana (Vehicle/Substance Taken With)
The medium used to deliver the herb enhances its effect:
- **Warm water:** General use; promotes absorption
- **Warm milk:** Vata-pacifying; nourishing; best for rasayana (rejuvenative) herbs
- **Ghee:** Pitta-pacifying; nourishing; carries herbs to deep tissues
- **Honey:** Kapha-pacifying; scraping; astringent; never heat honey
- **Rock salt water:** Kapha-reducing; stimulates digestion

### General Safety Notes
- Start with low doses and increase gradually
- Most herbs are taken after food unless otherwise specified (digestive herbs are taken before)
- Consult a practitioner for pregnancy, children under 7, and chronic conditions
- Herbs with strong Ushna virya should be used cautiously in Pitta individuals and summer
- Herbs with strong Sheeta virya should be used cautiously in Vata individuals and winter
- Triphala and Amalaki are safe for daily, long-term use by most people
