# Doshas: The Three Bioenergies

The three doshas -- Vata, Pitta, and Kapha -- are the fundamental forces governing all physiological and psychological activity. Every person has a unique ratio (Prakriti); disease arises when current state (Vikriti) deviates from constitution.

---

## Vata (Air + Ether) -- The Principle of Movement

### Qualities (Guna)
Dry, light, cold, rough, subtle, mobile, clear

### Physical Characteristics
- Thin, lean frame; difficulty gaining weight
- Dry skin and hair, brittle nails
- Cold hands and feet, poor circulation
- Irregular appetite and digestion
- Tendency toward constipation
- Quick movements, restlessness

### Mental/Emotional Tendencies
- Creative, enthusiastic, quick-thinking
- Learns fast, forgets fast
- Prone to anxiety, fear, and worry
- Irregular routines and habits
- Light, interrupted sleep

### Common Imbalances and Symptoms
- Anxiety, insomnia, nervousness
- Dry skin, cracked heels, brittle hair
- Constipation, gas, bloating
- Joint cracking, lower back pain
- Tremors, muscle spasms
- Fatigue from overstimulation

### Balancing Foods (Tastes/Rasa)
- Sweet (Madhura): rice, wheat, dairy, sweet fruits
- Sour (Amla): citrus, yogurt, fermented foods
- Salty (Lavana): seaweed, mineral salt
- Favor warm, moist, grounding foods; cooked grains, root vegetables, ghee, warm milk with nutmeg

### Aggravating Foods
- Raw salads, dry crackers, popcorn
- Bitter greens, astringent teas
- Cold beverages and ice
- Caffeine and stimulants
- Pungent spices (chili, cayenne)

### Balancing Herbs
- Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) -- grounding, strengthens nerves
- Shatavari (Asparagus racemosus) -- nourishing, moistening
- Bala (Sida cordifolia) -- strengthens muscles, calms Vata
- Tagara (Valeriana wallichii) -- promotes sleep
- Licorice (Glycyrrhiza glabra) -- demulcent, soothing

### Lifestyle Practices
- Maintain consistent daily routine (same sleep/wake times)
- Abhyanga (self-massage) with warm sesame oil daily
- Gentle, grounding exercise: yoga, walking, tai chi
- Warm baths; saunas are acceptable but avoid excess dry heat
- Minimize travel and sensory overstimulation
- Grounding activities: gardening, crafting, time in nature

### Seasonal Considerations
- Most aggravated in autumn (Sharad) and early winter -- dry, cold, windy
- Requires extra nourishment and oil application during these seasons
- Less vulnerable in spring (Kapha season) when moisture is abundant

## Pitta (Fire + Water) -- The Principle of Transformation

### Qualities (Guna)
Hot, sharp, light, oily, liquid, spreading

### Physical Characteristics
- Medium build, muscular, good metabolism
- Warm body temperature, prone to sweating
- Ruddy or flushed complexion
- Sensitive skin, prone to rashes/inflammation
- Strong appetite, can't skip meals
- Regular elimination, tendency toward loose stools

### Mental/Emotional Tendencies
- Intelligent, focused, goal-oriented
- Natural leaders, decisive
- Prone to anger, irritability, criticism
- Competitive, perfectionist
- Moderate sleep; may wake in the night

### Common Imbalances and Symptoms
- Acid reflux, heartburn, ulcers
- Skin rashes, hives, acne, inflammation
- Anger, impatience, burnout
- Excessive body heat, hot flashes
- Migraines, bloodshot eyes
- Diarrhea, hemorrhoids

### Balancing Foods (Tastes/Rasa)
- Sweet (Madhura): milk, ghee, coconut, sweet grains, melons
- Bitter (Tikta): leafy greens, turmeric, neem, fenugreek
- Astringent (Kashaya): beans, legumes, pomegranate, green tea
- Favor cool or room-temperature foods; avoid eating when angry

### Aggravating Foods
- Spicy food, chili peppers, garlic, onion
- Fermented foods (sour), vinegar, citrus excess
- Alcohol, coffee, chocolate
- Fried foods and excess oil
- Tomatoes, eggplant

### Balancing Herbs
- Amalaki (Emblica officinalis) -- cooling, antioxidant
- Shatavari (Asparagus racemosus) -- cooling, nourishing
- Brahmi (Bacopa monnieri) -- calms mind, reduces mental heat
- Guduchi (Tinospora cordifolia) -- cooling, supports liver
- Coriander, fennel, mint -- cooling digestive spices

### Lifestyle Practices
- Avoid excessive heat; exercise during cooler hours (early morning, evening)
- Swimming and water sports are ideal
- Moonlight walks; avoid midday sun
- Cultivate patience and compassion practices
- Wear cooling colors: white, blue, green, silver
- Avoid competitive or high-pressure situations when possible

### Seasonal Considerations
- Most aggravated in summer (Grishma) -- intense heat
- Favor cooling foods, coconut oil abhyanga, moonlight exposure
- Strong in late spring; monitor for skin inflammation

## Kapha (Earth + Water) -- The Principle of Structure

### Qualities (Guna)
Heavy, slow, cool, oily, smooth, dense, soft, stable

### Physical Characteristics
- Solid, sturdy build; gains weight easily
- Smooth, moist, pale skin
- Thick hair, strong nails
- Slow but steady digestion
- Deep, prolonged sleep
- Good stamina but slow to initiate activity

### Mental/Emotional Tendencies
- Calm, steady, loyal, compassionate
- Good long-term memory
- Prone to attachment, lethargy, depression
- Resistant to change
- Content but can be complacent

### Common Imbalances and Symptoms
- Weight gain, obesity
- Congestion, excess mucus, sinusitis
- Sluggish digestion, feeling heavy after meals
- Water retention, edema
- Diabetes, high cholesterol
- Lethargy, lack of motivation

### Balancing Foods (Tastes/Rasa)
- Pungent (Katu): ginger, black pepper, chili, mustard
- Bitter (Tikta): greens, turmeric, fenugreek, aloe
- Astringent (Kashaya): beans, pomegranate, cranberries, green tea
- Favor light, warm, well-spiced foods; minimize dairy and sweets

### Aggravating Foods
- Dairy products (cheese, milk, yogurt)
- Refined sugar, sweets, pastries
- Fried and heavy foods
- Cold drinks, ice cream
- Excessive wheat, rice, and heavy grains

### Balancing Herbs
- Trikatu (ginger + black pepper + long pepper) -- stimulates digestion
- Guggulu (Commiphora mukul) -- reduces kapha, supports metabolism
- Punarnava (Boerhavia diffusa) -- diuretic, reduces water retention
- Turmeric (Curcuma longa) -- anti-inflammatory, stimulates agni
- Chitrak (Plumbago zeylanica) -- powerful digestive stimulant

### Lifestyle Practices
- Vigorous, regular exercise daily (most dosha needs it)
- Wake before 6:00 AM; avoid sleeping in
- Dry brushing (Garshana) before abhyanga with light mustard or sunflower oil
- Seek new experiences, travel, variety
- Declutter living spaces regularly
- Stimulating activities: dancing, challenging work, active socializing

### Seasonal Considerations
- Most aggravated in spring (Vasanta) and monsoon (Varsha) -- heavy, moist, cool
- Kapha-reducing diet and vigorous exercise are critical in these seasons
- Strongest in winter; this is their natural season of stability
