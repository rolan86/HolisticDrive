# Depression

## Holistic Perspective
Depression is viewed holistically as a systemic condition with roots in neuroinflammation, gut-brain axis dysfunction, HPA axis dysregulation, nutrient depletion, and psychosocial factors -- rather than simply a "chemical imbalance." The inflammatory model of depression recognizes that chronic systemic inflammation drives neuroinflammation, altering neurotransmitter metabolism and neural plasticity. A holistic approach addresses the whole person: biological terrain, emotional landscape, social connections, sense of meaning, and lifestyle patterns that either sustain or alleviate the condition.

## Common Patterns
- Persistent low mood, anhedonia (loss of pleasure), fatigue, brain fog, difficulty concentrating, sleep disruption (insomnia or hypersomnia)
- Physical symptoms: body aches, digestive issues, appetite changes, heavy feeling in limbs, low libido
- Co-occurring: anxiety, chronic pain, IBS, thyroid disorders, autoimmune conditions, chronic fatigue, substance use
- Lab markers: elevated hs-CRP (inflammatory marker), elevated homocysteine, low vitamin D (25-OH below 30 ng/mL), low B12 (below 400 pg/mL functional range), low folate, low omega-3 index, elevated cortisol or flattened cortisol curve, low DHEA-S, thyroid dysfunction, low magnesium RBC, gut dysbiosis
- Body systems: nervous system, immune system (chronic inflammation), endocrine system (HPA axis, thyroid), digestive system (gut-brain axis)

## Holistic Approaches

### Nutrition
- Anti-inflammatory diet as foundation: Mediterranean or modified Paleo pattern emphasizing whole foods, omega-3s, and phytonutrients
- **Omega-3 fatty acids:** 2-4g EPA daily from fish oil has comparable efficacy to SSRIs in some trials for major depression; wild-caught salmon, sardines, mackerel 4-5x weekly
- **Probiotic-rich foods:** daily sauerkraut, kimchi, kefir, or miso -- specific strains (Lactobacillus rhamnosus, Bifidobacterium longum) show antidepressant effects via the gut-brain axis
- **Vitamin D:** 2000-5000 IU daily with K2; maintain levels at 50-70 ng/mL; deficiency is one of the most common modifiable risk factors
- Increase zinc-rich foods: pumpkin seeds, oysters, grass-fed beef, lentils (zinc deficiency impairs neurotransmitter synthesis)
- **SAMe (S-adenosylmethionine):** 400-1600 mg daily on empty stomach; supports methylation and neurotransmitter production; avoid with bipolar disorder
- Dark leafy greens daily (folate source): spinach, kale, chard; folate deficiency directly impairs serotonin synthesis
- Eliminate processed foods, refined sugar, and industrial seed oils -- these drive neuroinflammation

### Herbal Medicine
- **St. John's Wort (Hypericum perforatum):** 300 mg standardized to 0.3% hypericin, 3x daily; strongest evidence base of any herb for mild-to-moderate depression; interacts with MANY medications (SSRIs, birth control, blood thinners) -- always check interactions
- **Turmeric/Curcumin:** 500-1000 mg standardized curcumin with piperine daily; anti-neuroinflammatory action; efficacy comparable to fluoxetine in trials
- **Saffron (Crocus sativus):** 30 mg saffron extract daily; comparable efficacy to fluoxetine and imipramine in trials; also improves sleep
- **Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera):** 300-600 mg KSM-66 twice daily; particularly for depression with anxiety and stress components
- **Rhodiola (Rhodiola rosea):** 200-400 mg standardized to 3% rosavins; for depression with fatigue, apathy, and burnout; morning use only
- **Lavender (Lavandula angustifolia):** Silexan 80 mg daily; for depression comorbid with anxiety
- **Lion's Mane mushroom (Hericium erinaceus):** 1000-3000 mg daily; stimulates nerve growth factor (NGF) and supports neuroplasticity
- **Maca (Lepidium meyenii):** 1.5-3 g daily; for depression associated with hormonal imbalance, particularly perimenopause

### Ayurvedic Perspective
- **Kapha** depression (lethargy, weight gain, excessive sleep, apathy, attachment): stimulating, warming, and drying practices; trikatu spice mix, dry brushing, vigorous exercise, early rising before 6 AM, fasting from heavy foods
- **Vata** depression (anxiety, fear, insomnia, scattered thoughts, worry): grounding and nourishing practices; warm oil Abhyanga, shirodhara, warm spiced milk, ashwagandha, consistent routine
- **Pitta** depression (anger turned inward, irritability, self-criticism, judgment): cooling practices; brahmi oil head massage, bitters, moonlight walks, forgiveness practices
- **Brahmi (Bacopa monnieri):** 300-500 mg daily; medhya rasayana (brain rejuvenative) that enhances serotonin, dopamine, and GABA
- **Shankhpushpi (Convolvulus pluricaulis):** 1-2 tsp in warm milk at bedtime; classical Ayurvedic brain tonic for mood and memory
- **Chyawanprash** (1-2 tsp daily): systemic rejuvenative containing amla (vitamin C) and 40+ herbs for overall vitality
- **Panchakarma** detoxification therapies (supervised) can be transformative for depressive patterns, particularly Kapha-type

### Movement & Exercise
- **Exercise is one of the most potent antidepressants available** -- comparable to SSRIs in head-to-head trials for mild-to-moderate depression
- Aerobic exercise: 30-45 minutes, 4-5x weekly at moderate intensity; walking, cycling, swimming, dancing
- **Yoga:** particularly effective for depression; combines movement, breath, mindfulness, and social connection (group classes); Kripalu, Viniyoga, or gentle flow styles
- **Strength training** 2-3x weekly has independent antidepressant effects beyond aerobic exercise
- Outdoor exercise (green exercise) has additive benefits via sunlight exposure (vitamin D) and nature contact
- Consistency over intensity -- 20 minutes daily outperforms sporadic intense sessions

### Mind & Stress
- Address meaning, purpose, and social connection -- isolation and meaninglessness are as pathogenic as any biochemical factor
- **Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy (MBCT)** reduces depression relapse by 50% and is recommended by clinical guidelines as maintenance therapy
- **Behavioral activation:** schedule pleasurable and mastery activities even when motivation is absent; action precedes motivation
- **Gratitude practices** (journaling 3 specific things daily) have measurable effects on depressive symptoms within 2-4 weeks
- **Social rhythm therapy:** regular sleep, meal, and activity times stabilize circadian rhythms which directly impact mood
- Connection with community, nature, animals, creative expression, and spiritual practice all have evidence-based antidepressant effects

## Cross-Domain Connections
Depression management benefits from the **Mental Health Counselor** (for CBT, MBCT, EMDR, or psychodynamic therapy), **Nutrition Therapist** (for anti-inflammatory nutrition, gut-brain axis repair, and neurotransmitter precursor support), and **Mind-Body Practitioner** (for meditation, yoga therapy, breathwork, and somatic practices). The **Functional Medicine** specialist can investigate inflammatory, hormonal, and nutrient-related drivers. The **Herbalist** provides antidepressant botanicals with appropriate interaction screening.

## Red Flags
- Suicidal ideation, self-harm, or suicide plan -- immediate psychiatric evaluation; call 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline
- Psychotic symptoms: hallucinations, delusions, or disorganized thinking
- Severe functional impairment: inability to perform basic self-care, work, or maintain safety
- Bipolar features: periods of elevated mood, decreased need for sleep, grandiosity, or risky behavior (St. John's Wort and SAMe can trigger mania in bipolar disorder)
- Catatonia: immobility, mutism, or excessive purposeless movement
- Rapid onset or first episode after age 50 -- may indicate neurological, endocrine, or substance-related cause
