# Insomnia

## Holistic Perspective
Insomnia is viewed holistically not merely as a sleep disorder but as a symptom of systemic dysregulation -- an inability of the nervous system to transition from sympathetic dominance to parasympathetic rest. Root causes span circadian rhythm disruption, cortisol dysregulation, neurotransmitter imbalances, digestive distress, hormonal shifts, and unresolved emotional patterns. Effective holistic treatment addresses the entire sleep ecosystem: light exposure, temperature, timing, nutrition, nervous system state, and the mental-emotional landscape.

## Common Patterns
- Difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking too early (3-5 AM cortisol surge)
- Daytime fatigue, irritability, impaired concentration, increased pain sensitivity
- Racing thoughts at bedtime, physical tension, feeling "wired," or waking with heart pounding
- Co-occurring: anxiety, depression, hormonal imbalances (perimenopause, thyroid), chronic pain, gut issues, adrenal dysregulation
- Lab markers: disrupted cortisol rhythm (high evening cortisol on DUTCH or salivary tests), low melatonin, low magnesium RBC, low GABA, elevated homocysteine, thyroid dysfunction, vitamin D deficiency
- Body systems: nervous system, endocrine system (HPA axis, pineal gland, thyroid), digestive system, musculoskeletal system

## Holistic Approaches

### Nutrition
- **Tart cherry juice** (8 oz) or concentrate (2 tbsp) 1-2 hours before bed; natural source of melatonin and procyanidins shown to improve sleep duration by 84 minutes in clinical trials
- **Magnesium glycinate** 200-400 mg 30-60 minutes before bed; glycinate form is calming and well-tolerated
- Foods rich in tryptophan: turkey, pastured eggs, pumpkin seeds, tart cherries, and dark chocolate (small amount, early evening)
- Complex carbohydrates with dinner (sweet potato, quinoa, brown rice) to facilitate tryptophan transport across the blood-brain barrier
- **Passionflower tea** 1-2 cups in the evening; increases GABA levels
- Avoid alcohol -- it fragments sleep architecture despite initial sedation; causes rebound awakening
- Last meal 2-3 hours before bed; going to sleep too full or too hungry disrupts sleep
- Eliminate caffeine by 12 PM (caffeine half-life is 5-7 hours; quarter-life can extend to 12+ hours)

### Herbal Medicine
- **Valerian (Valeriana officinalis):** 300-600 mg standardized extract 30-60 min before bed; acts as a mild sedative via GABA modulation; combine with lemon balm for enhanced effect
- **Passionflower (Passiflora incarnata):** 250-500 mg extract or 2-4 mL tincture before bed; anxiolytic and sleep-inducing; safe for long-term use
- **Lavender (Lavandula angustifolia):** oral capsules (Silexan, 80 mg) have comparable efficacy to benzodiazepines in trials; also effective as essential oil (diffusion or pillow spray)
- **Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera):** 300 mg KSM-66 extract twice daily if cortisol dysregulation is contributing to insomnia
- **Chamomile (Matricaria recutita):** 2-3 cups of strong tea (2-3 bags steeped 10+ min) in the evening; apigenin content binds GABA receptors
- **Ziziphus (Ziziphus jujuba):** 5-10 g in decoction; Traditional Chinese Medicine remedy for restlessness and disturbed sleep
- **California Poppy (Eschscholzia californica):** 1-2 mL tincture before bed for gentle sedation without grogginess
- **Magnolia bark (Magnolia officinalis):** 200-400 mg standardized honokiol extract; modulates GABA and reduces cortisol; avoid with benzodiazepines

### Ayurvedic Perspective
- **Vata** insomnia (difficulty falling, restless mind, anxiety): warm sesame oil foot massage (Padabhyanga) before bed, warm spiced milk with nutmeg (1/8 tsp) and ashwagandha
- **Pitta** insomnia (waking between 2-4 AM with anger or racing thoughts): cool coconut oil head massage (Shiroabhyanga), brahmi oil on temples, shatavari in warm milk, bitter herbs
- **Kapha** insomnia (waking feeling heavy, unrefreshed, excessive sleeping): kapha-reducing diet, stimulating herbs (ginger, punarnava), earlier and lighter dinner
- **Nasya** with warm sesame or brahmi ghee (3-5 drops per nostril) before bed calms the mind and nervous system
- **Shirodhara** (warm oil stream on forehead) -- profoundly effective for Vata and Pitta insomnia; available through Ayurvedic practitioners
- Favor a consistent bedtime routine (Dinacharya) with sleep by 10 PM; Pitta time (10 PM - 2 AM) is the "second wind" that makes sleep harder if missed

### Movement & Exercise
- Morning sunlight exposure (10-20 min within first hour of waking) to set circadian rhythm and suppress melatonin
- **Evening walks** (20-30 min) in dim light post-dinner; moderate exercise improves sleep quality, but avoid vigorous exercise within 3 hours of bedtime
- Restorative yoga before bed: Legs-Up-The-Wall (Viparita Karani), Supported Child's Pose, Supta Baddha Konasana (5-10 min each)
- **Progressive Muscle Relaxation (PMR)** -- systematically tense and release each muscle group (15-20 min) to release physical tension
- Gentle stretching or foam rolling in the evening to address muscular tension that keeps the body alert

### Mind & Stress
- **Sleep hygiene is not enough** for most people with chronic insomnia -- nervous system regulation is the deeper work
- **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I)** is the gold-standard non-pharmacological treatment with sustained efficacy
- **NSDR (Non-Sleep Deep Rest)** or Yoga Nidra recordings (20-30 min) can be used as a sleep transition practice
- 4-7-8 breathing (inhale 4 sec, hold 7 sec, exhale 8 sec) activates the parasympathetic nervous system at bedtime
- Journaling "brain dump" (writing down worries, to-do lists) 1-2 hours before bed externalizes racing thoughts
- Temperature manipulation: warm bath/shower 1-2 hours before bed, then cool bedroom (65-68 F / 18-20 C)

## Cross-Domain Connections
Insomnia management benefits from the **Mind-Body Practitioner** (for CBT-I, somatic therapy, breathwork, and nervous system regulation), **Nutrition Therapist** (for sleep-supportive nutrition and neurotransmitter precursor support), and **Herbalist** (for sedative and adaptogenic botanical protocols). The **Functional Medicine** specialist can investigate underlying hormonal or metabolic drivers. Persistent insomnia tied to psychological patterns warrants the **Mental Health Counselor**.

## Red Flags
- Insomnia lasting more than 3 months with significant daytime impairment
- Sleep apnea signs: loud snoring, gasping during sleep, morning headaches, observed pauses in breathing
- Frequent nighttime urination (nocturia) -- may indicate diabetes, prostate issues, or heart conditions
- Insomnia accompanied by tremors, rapid heartbeat, significant weight change, or heat intolerance (thyroid or cardiac evaluation needed)
- Hallucinations, severe confusion, or microsleeps while driving
- Dependence on sleep medications or alcohol to sleep with increasing doses needed
