# Migraines

## Holistic Perspective
Migraines are understood holistically as a neurological and vascular condition with multifactorial triggers spanning genetics, hormonal fluctuations, gut health, nutrient status, environmental sensitivities, and nervous system dysregulation. Rather than simply a "bad headache," migraines represent a cascade of cortical spreading depression, trigeminal nerve activation, and neurogenic inflammation. A holistic approach identifies and addresses individual triggers, restores neurological resilience, reduces systemic inflammation, and builds a foundation of nervous system stability.

## Common Patterns
- Unilateral, pulsating headache of moderate to severe intensity, often with nausea, vomiting, and sensitivity to light, sound, or smell
- Aura in approximately 25% of cases: visual disturbances, tingling, or speech changes preceding the headache
- Prodrome phase (hours to days before): mood changes, food cravings, yawning, neck stiffness, fatigue, brain fog
- Co-occurring: anxiety, depression, IBS, fibromyalgia, endometriosis, insomnia, TMJ disorders, PMS
- Lab markers: low magnesium RBC (most common finding), low vitamin D, low CoQ10, elevated homocysteine (MTHFR mutations), elevated hs-CRP, food sensitivity panels (common triggers: gluten, dairy, histamine-rich foods), hormonal panel (estrogen fluctuations)
- Body systems: nervous system (brain, trigeminal nerve), vascular system, endocrine system (hormonal triggers), digestive system (gut-brain axis), musculoskeletal (cervical spine, jaw)

## Holistic Approaches

### Nutrition
- **Identify and eliminate dietary triggers** through an elimination diet; most common: aged cheeses, red wine, chocolate, cured meats (nitrates), MSG, artificial sweeteners, gluten, and dairy
- **Magnesium-rich foods daily:** dark leafy greens, pumpkin seeds (1/4 cup), almonds, black beans, avocados, dark chocolate (70%+, 1-2 squares)
- **Hydration:** adequate water intake (at least 2-3 liters daily); even mild dehydration is a common migraine trigger
- **Riboflavin (B2)-rich foods:** liver, eggs, almonds, milk, yogurt, spinach -- 400 mg daily supplementation has strong evidence for migraine prevention
- **CoQ10:** 100-300 mg daily; mitochondrial support -- migraine brains show impaired mitochondrial energy production
- **Omega-3 fatty acids:** 2-3g EPA/DHA daily; reduce neurogenic inflammation and platelet aggregation
- Eat regular meals -- fasting or blood sugar drops are potent triggers; never skip meals
- Consider a low-histamine diet if histamine-rich foods (fermented foods, aged cheese, wine, vinegar) are triggers

### Herbal Medicine
- **Butterbur (Petasites hybridus):** 75 mg standardized extract (PA-free) twice daily; strongest evidence base of any herb for migraine prevention -- reduces frequency by up to 50%; ensure PA-free (pyrrolizidine alkaloid-free) preparation
- **Feverfew (Tanacetum parthenium):** 50-100 mg dried leaf capsules daily or 2-3 fresh leaves daily; inhibits serotonin release from platelets and reduces inflammation; must be used consistently for 2-3 months for full effect
- **Ginger (Zingiber officinale):** 250-500 mg ginger powder or 1 tsp fresh ginger at onset of migraine; comparable efficacy to sumatriptan for acute migraine in trials; anti-nausea and anti-inflammatory
- **Magnesium glycinate:** 400-600 mg daily (split doses); most effective preventive supplement for migraines; glycinate form for better absorption and fewer GI effects
- **Peppermint oil:** topical application (10% menthol solution) to temples and forehead at migraine onset; cooling analgesic effect
- **Willow Bark (Salix alba):** 120-240 mg salicin for acute pain; natural precursor to aspirin with fewer GI effects; avoid with aspirin allergy or blood thinners
- **Cannabis (CBD/THC):** emerging evidence for acute migraine relief; CBD 25-50 mg with THC 5-10 mg at onset (where legally available)
- **Lavender oil:** inhalation (2-4 drops on a tissue or diffuser) at migraine onset; one study showed 71% response rate

### Ayurvedic Perspective
- Primarily a **Vata** imbalance (pain, irregularity, anxiety, constipation) with **Pitta** involvement (light/sound sensitivity, nausea, heat, anger) during acute phase
- **Godanti Bhasma:** classical Ayurvedic preparation for headaches; 125-250 mg with honey 2x daily during acute phase
- **Shirashooladi Vajra Rasa:** traditional Ayurvedic tablet formulation (125-250 mg 2x daily) for migraines with Vata-Pitta features
- **Nasya therapy:** application of medicated oil (Anu taila or Brahmi ghee, 3-5 drops per nostril) clears sinuses and calms the head region
- **Shirodhara** (warm oil stream on forehead) during prodrome or between attacks for prevention and nervous system calming
- Apply warm sesame oil or Bala oil to scalp and soles of feet; gentle head massage (Shiroabhyanga) with Brahmi oil
- Avoid known trigger foods; favor warm, cooked, grounding foods for Vata balance
- **Triphala** (1/2 tsp at bedtime) for regular elimination -- constipation is a common migraine trigger in Ayurvedic framework

### Movement & Exercise
- **Regular moderate exercise** reduces migraine frequency by 25-40% in studies -- comparable to topiramate prevention
- Walking, swimming, and cycling are ideal; avoid high-impact activities during prodrome or attack
- **Yoga:** specific poses that release neck and shoulder tension (standing forward fold, seated twist, supported bridge) are particularly beneficial
- **Cervical spine mobility work:** gentle neck stretches and myofascial release of suboccipital muscles and trapezius; many migraines originate from cervical tension
- Avoid vigorous exercise during a migraine attack; rest in a dark, quiet room instead
- Consistent exercise schedule matters more than intensity -- sporadic intense workouts can trigger attacks

### Mind & Stress
- **Stress is the most commonly reported migraine trigger** -- both acute stress and the relaxation period following stress ("let-down" migraines)
- **Biofeedback** (particularly thermal biofeedback) has FDA approval for migraine prevention and strong clinical evidence; trains patients to raise hand temperature (vasodilation) and reduce muscle tension
- **Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR)** reduces migraine frequency, severity, and medication use
- Progressive Muscle Relaxation (PMR) during prodrome can prevent full attack development
- **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy** addresses catastrophic thinking about pain, reduces migraine-related disability, and improves coping
- Maintain a consistent sleep schedule -- both too little and too much sleep are triggers; aim for 7-9 hours at the same time daily

## Cross-Domain Connections
Migraine management benefits from the **Nutrition Therapist** (for trigger identification, elimination diets, and migraine-preventive nutrition), **Mind-Body Practitioner** (for biofeedback, meditation, breathwork, and nervous system regulation), and **Herbalist** (for preventive and acute botanical protocols). The **Functional Medicine** specialist can investigate MTHFR mutations, hormonal patterns, and nutrient deficiencies. The **Bodyworker / Massage Therapist** addresses cervical and cranial tension patterns. If structural issues are dominant, a referral for physical therapy or craniosacral therapy may be warranted.

## Red Flags
- "Thunderclap" headache -- sudden onset of maximum-intensity headache within seconds (rule out subarachnoid hemorrhage)
- New headache pattern after age 50, or significant change in established pattern
- Headache with fever, stiff neck, confusion, seizures, or double vision (meningitis or encephalitis)
- Progressive worsening with neurological deficits: weakness, numbness, speech difficulty, vision loss
- Headache following head trauma
- Migraine with prolonged aura (>60 minutes) or aura without headache in new onset
- Headache that is always on the same side and progressively worsening (rule out structural cause)
