# Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS)

## Holistic Perspective
IBS is viewed holistically as a disorder of the gut-brain axis, where dysregulation of the enteric nervous system, altered gut microbiota, and impaired intestinal permeability converge. Emotional stress, dietary triggers, and chronic inflammation all play interconnected roles rather than any single isolated cause. Addressing IBS requires a systems-level approach that considers digestion, nervous system regulation, and emotional wellbeing simultaneously.

## Common Patterns
- Bloating, gas, abdominal cramping, and irregular bowel habits (diarrhea, constipation, or alternating)
- Co-occurring symptoms: fatigue, brain fog, anxiety, skin issues, food sensitivities
- Lab markers: elevated calprotectin (to rule out IBD), zonulin (intestinal permeability), cortisol dysregulation, low-grade inflammatory markers (hs-CRP), SIBO breath tests, comprehensive stool analysis (microbiome imbalance, low secretory IgA)
- Body systems: digestive system, nervous system (enteric and central), immune system, endocrine system (HPA axis)

## Holistic Approaches

### Nutrition
- Identify and eliminate personal trigger foods; common culprits include FODMAPs (garlic, onion, wheat, certain dairy, beans, apples), caffeine, alcohol, and processed foods
- Incorporate prebiotic-rich foods gradually: cooked and cooled potatoes, asparagus tips, green bananas, flaxseed (1-2 tbsp daily)
- Include bone broth or collagen peptides (10-15g daily) to support gut lining integrity
- Emphasize omega-3-rich foods: wild-caught salmon, sardines, chia seeds, walnuts (anti-inflammatory)
- Add fermented foods cautiously and individually: sauerkraut (1-2 tbsp), coconut yogurt, kefir -- introduce slowly to assess tolerance
- Cook vegetables thoroughly; raw salads can be irritating during flare-ups
- Ginger tea (fresh ginger steeped 10 min) before meals to stimulate digestive fire
- Peppermint oil enteric-coated capsules (180-225 mg, 2x daily between meals) for spasm relief

### Herbal Medicine
- **Peppermint (Mentha x piperita):** enteric-coated capsules 180-225 mg 2x daily between meals for intestinal spasm and bloating; avoid with GERD or hiatal hernia
- **Slippery Elm (Ulmus rubra):** 400-500 mg capsules or 1 tsp powder in warm water before meals as a demulcent to soothe irritated mucosa
- **Marshmallow Root (Althea officinalis):** 2-4 g daily as a cold infusion or capsule; coats and protects the gut lining
- **Turmeric (Curcuma longa):** 500-1000 mg standardized curcumin extract daily with black pepper (piperine) for anti-inflammatory action; caution with blood thinners
- **Chamomile (Matricaria recutita):** 2-3 cups of strong tea daily for nervous digestive tension and cramping
- **Berberine-containing herbs** (Oregon grape, goldenseal): 500 mg 2-3x daily short-term (2-4 weeks) for antimicrobial support if SIBO is suspected; monitor liver enzymes
- **Fennel (Foeniculum vulgare):** 1 tsp crushed seeds steeped 10 min, 2-3x daily after meals for carminative relief
- **Deglycyrrhizinated Licorice (DGL):** 2 chewable tablets (380 mg) before meals to support mucosal healing

### Ayurvedic Perspective
- Primarily a **Vata** imbalance (gas, bloating, irregular bowels, anxiety); secondary **Pitta** involvement when inflammation and burning are present
- **Hingwashtak churna** (1/4 tsp with ghee before meals) is a classic formula for Vata-type digestive weakness and gas
- Cook with digestive spices: cumin, coriander, fennel, ginger, and turmeric (collectively known as CCF tea when steeped together)
- Favor warm, cooked, well-spiced foods; avoid cold, raw, dry, and rough foods that aggravate Vata
- Daily **Abhyanga** (self-massage with warm sesame oil) calms Vata and supports the nervous system
- **Triphala** (1/2 tsp before bed in warm water) as a gentle bowel regulator and digestive tonic

### Movement & Exercise
- Gentle yoga: poses such as Apanasana (knees-to-chest), Supine Twist, and Cat-Cow to massage the abdomen and encourage peristalsis
- Daily walking (20-30 minutes) after meals to support gastric motility without overstimulating the system
- Avoid high-impact exercise during flare-ups; vigorous running can exacerbate symptoms
- Tai chi or qigong practices that combine gentle movement with breath regulation and vagal tone activation
- Restorative yoga or yoga nidra (20-30 min) to downregulate the stress response that directly impacts gut function

### Mind & Stress
- The gut-brain axis is bidirectional; chronic stress directly alters intestinal motility, permeability, and microbiome composition
- **Vagus nerve exercises:** humming, gargling, cold water face immersion to stimulate vagal tone and improve gut-brain communication
- **Diaphragmatic breathing** (5-10 min before meals) shifts the body from sympathetic to parasympathetic state for optimal digestion
- **Gut-directed hypnotherapy** has strong evidence for IBS symptom reduction; consider working with a trained practitioner
- Journaling or somatic tracking to identify emotional patterns and food-symptom correlations

## Cross-Domain Connections
IBS typically benefits from collaboration between the **Nutrition Therapist** (for elimination diet protocols and gut-healing nutrition plans), **Mind-Body Practitioner** (for stress modulation, gut-brain axis interventions, and somatic therapy), and **Herbalist** (for targeted botanical support). When SIBO or dysbiosis is suspected, coordination with the **Functional Medicine** specialist for advanced testing and antimicrobial protocols is valuable. Persistent psychological comorbidities warrant referral to the **Mental Health Counselor**.

## Red Flags
- Unintentional weight loss of 10+ pounds without trying
- Blood in stool or black, tarry stools
- Onset after age 50 (warrants colonoscopy to rule out malignancy)
- Nocturnal symptoms that wake the person from sleep (atypical for IBS)
- Persistent fever, severe pain, or progressive worsening of symptoms
- Iron deficiency anemia or elevated inflammatory markers (calprotectin, CRP) suggesting IBD rather than IBS
